Why Is My YouTube Video Not Getting Views?

J
Jayson Hines September 19, 2026 · 7 min read

Short answer: your video is almost never being hidden. It is either not getting shown to many people, or it is getting shown and nobody is clicking. Those are two completely different problems with two completely different fixes, and YouTube Studio tells you which one you have in about thirty seconds. Open the video, go to Analytics, then the Reach tab, and look at impressions and click through rate before you change anything else.

I have this conversation a lot with local business owners who finally started posting video. They shoot something decent, upload it, refresh the page for three days, and the counter sits at 14. Then they assume YouTube throttled them. Usually it is something way more boring than that.

Are you getting impressions, or getting clicks?

This is the whole diagnosis. Impressions means how many times YouTube put your thumbnail in front of a human being. Click through rate is what percentage of those people actually tapped it.

If impressions are very low, say under a few hundred, YouTube is not distributing the video. That is a topic and channel problem. Nobody is searching for it, and YouTube does not yet have an audience it feels confident showing you to.

If impressions are healthy but click through rate is under about 2 percent, distribution is fine and your packaging is the problem. The thumbnail and title are not making anyone curious. That one you can fix this afternoon without reshooting a thing.

And if both look okay but views still stall, scroll down to average view duration. If people are leaving in the first 30 seconds, YouTube stops recommending it, because it judges the video by what viewers did, not by what you uploaded.

Why does YouTube stop pushing a video after a day?

YouTube shows a new upload to a small group first. Your subscribers, people who watch similar stuff, whoever it thinks might care. Then it watches. If those people click and stay, it widens the circle. If they skip past it or bail early, it quietly stops.

So a video that got 200 views on day one and then flatlined did not get punished. It got tested and did not pass. The fix is not to delete it, it is to figure out which part of the test it lost.

One thing people get wrong here: you can change the thumbnail and title on a video that already published. YouTube will retest it. I have seen a video sitting dead for two weeks pick back up after nothing changed but the thumbnail.

Did you accidentally block your own video?

Before you blame the algorithm, run through these. I would guess a quarter of the "no views" problems I get asked about are one of these five.

1: Visibility is set to Unlisted or Private. Unlisted videos do not show in search, browse, or suggested at all. They only work if you hand someone the link.

2: It is still scheduled. If you set a publish time and the date has not hit, the video is sitting there invisible.

3: Audience is set to "Yes, it's made for kids." That setting turns off recommendations, notifications, and comments on the video. It is easy to click by accident when you are rushing through the upload screen, and it will gut your reach.

4: It is still processing. Higher resolution versions can take a while after upload, and until those finish the video looks rough to anyone who clicks it. If you share it immediately, early viewers get a blurry version and leave.

5: Age restriction or a limited state. Check the Restrictions column in YouTube Studio. If something got flagged, it shows up right there in the video list.

Is your topic something anyone is actually searching for?

This is the one nobody wants to hear. A lot of business videos get no views because no human being was ever going to type that phrase into YouTube.

"Meet our team" and "2026 company update" have zero search demand. Nobody wakes up wanting those. "How much does a metal roof cost in Ohio" has demand every single day. Same camera, same fifteen minutes of your time, wildly different outcome.

Go type your topic into the YouTube search bar and look at what autocompletes. Those suggestions are real queries real people type. If nothing close to your title shows up, that is your answer, and no thumbnail is going to rescue it.

For local businesses this is actually good news, because the competition on local how-to questions is thin. Most national channels will not bother answering "what does a furnace tune up include in Toledo." You can own that. If you want help mapping out which questions your customers are actually asking, that is a big part of what we do in our social media and content services for local businesses.

What about Shorts and search specifically?

Shorts run on a different distribution system than long form, so if your problem is specifically Shorts sitting at zero, I broke that down in why your YouTube Shorts are not getting views.

And if the issue is that your channel itself cannot be found when people type your business name, that is a separate fix. See why your YouTube channel is not showing up in search.

What I would actually do this week

Pull up your last five videos and check the Reach tab on each one. Write down impressions and click through rate. You will see a pattern fast, and it will almost always be one of three things. Either nothing is getting impressions, or plenty of impressions with a weak click rate, or good clicks with people leaving in the first half minute.

If impressions are the problem, change what you make. Answer a question people type. Put that exact question in the title.

If clicks are the problem, redo thumbnails on your existing videos. Big readable text, one clear subject, a face if you have one. Test it at phone size, because that is where almost everyone sees it.

If retention is the problem, cut your first 20 seconds. Most business videos open with a logo animation and a slow greeting. Start with the answer instead and earn the rest.

Then give it 28 days before you decide anything. YouTube is slow in a way Facebook and Instagram are not, and plenty of videos that look dead in week one start pulling steady search traffic in month two. That slow build is the whole reason it is worth doing.

Common questions

Why did my YouTube video stop getting views after the first day?
That is normal. YouTube pushes a new upload to a small test group, watches how they respond, and then either widens the reach or stops. If the test group clicked at a low rate or left early, distribution slows down within a day or two. The video is not blocked, it just did not earn more.

How many views should a new YouTube channel get?
A brand new channel with no subscribers commonly sits in the single digits or low double digits per video for the first several uploads. YouTube has no watch history for your channel yet, so it has nobody to recommend you to. That usually changes somewhere between upload 10 and upload 30, not on upload 3.

Does deleting and reuploading a YouTube video help it get views?
Almost never, and you lose whatever data the first upload gathered. If the thumbnail or title was the problem, change those on the existing video instead. YouTube lets you swap both at any time and it will retest the video with the new packaging.

Does the Made for Kids setting stop my video from getting views?
It limits it a lot. When a video is marked as made for kids, YouTube turns off personalized recommendations, notifications, comments, and a few other features on it. If you set that by mistake on a business video, change it in YouTube Studio under the video's Audience setting.

How long should I wait before deciding a YouTube video failed?
Give it about 28 days before you judge it. Some videos are slow starters that pick up through search or suggested weeks later, especially how-to and local topics. What you should check on day two is the reach data, because that tells you whether the problem was impressions, clicks, or retention.
