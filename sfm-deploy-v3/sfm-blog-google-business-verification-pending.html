Why Is My Google Business Profile Verification Still Pending?

J
Jayson Hines September 18, 2026 · 7 min read

Short answer: if your Google Business Profile still says pending, it is almost always one of four things. Google's review window has not actually run out yet, your video missed something the reviewer needed to see, your business details do not match what Google finds elsewhere on the web, or you are in a category that gets hand checked. Pending is not the same as rejected, so do not tear the profile down and start over.

I get this call a lot. Somebody opens a shop in Maumee or a service business out of their house in Perrysburg, they do the whole video walkthrough, and then nothing. No email, no rejection, just that gray little banner sitting there while their competitors show up on the map and they do not.

So here is what is really going on and what to do about it.

How long is Google Business Profile verification supposed to take?

Google's own number is up to 5 business days for a video review. Business days, not calendar days, so a submission on a Friday afternoon before a holiday can eat more than a week before anybody has even looked at it. Postcard verification runs longer because it has to physically show up in your mailbox.

If you are inside that window, you are not stuck. You are just waiting, and refreshing the dashboard ten times a day does not speed it up.

Past 5 business days with no email and no change, now you have a real problem worth chasing.

Why does it still say pending after I already did the video?

Because the video getting accepted and the video getting approved are two different steps. Your phone uploads it fine, the screen says thanks, and then a reviewer looks at it later and decides whether it proved what Google wanted proved.

The most common reason it quietly fails is that the recording never showed the reviewer that you actually run the place. People film the building and the sign and stop there. That proves a building exists. It does not prove it is yours.

What Google actually wants to see in the verification video

The rules are specific, and most of the failures I see are people breaking one of them without knowing it.

It has to be recorded live in the Google Maps app on a phone, signed into the same Google account that manages the profile. You cannot record it on a camera and upload it, you cannot do it from a desktop, and you cannot record it now and send it tomorrow. It also has to be one continuous take with no edits, and at least 30 seconds. Somewhere in the 30 to 60 second range is the sweet spot.

What you walk through matters just as much. Start outside with something that ties you to the address, a street sign, a building number, your own signage. Then get the entrance. Then go inside and show the working space. Then finish with the part everybody skips: proof you have the keys to the place. Unlocking the door, opening the register, using the equipment, pulling out branded gear or paperwork with your business name on it.

Service area businesses run out of a home get a different version of this. You are not showing a storefront because you do not have one. You show the branded truck or trailer, the tools in it, the equipment you work with, and something that connects you to the address on file.

A few things will get you sent back around every time. Do not film people, faces, or voices. Do not let a conversation run in the background. Do not point the camera at bank paperwork, tax documents, or a customer's information sitting on a desk. Record when the place is quiet.

Does my business info have to match everywhere else?

Yes, and this one catches good businesses constantly.

Google cross checks the name, address, and phone number on your profile against what it finds on your website, your social pages, and every directory that has ever scraped you. If your Google listing says Suite B and your website says nothing, or your phone number on Facebook is the old line from three years ago, that mismatch reads as a red flag to an automated system that is mostly trying to catch fake listings.

Before you chase support, open your own website and make sure the name, the full address, and the phone number sitting on your contact page are character for character what you put in Google. Fix the site first, then let Google look again. Getting this consistent is the same work that makes you rank once you are verified, and it is part of what we do in our local search and social media services for businesses around Toledo and the surrounding towns.

When should I appeal, and how?

Wait out the 5 business days first. Then use the verification support flow inside your Google Business Profile dashboard, not the suspension tool.

That distinction matters more than it sounds like it should. The suspension appeals tool is built for profiles that were already live and then got suspended or restricted. It does nothing for a profile that is unverified and sitting in review, and I have watched people burn two weeks sending a verification problem down a pipe that was never going to answer it. If your profile was actually suspended, that is a different fix, and I wrote it up in why your Google Business Profile got suspended.

File one appeal. One. Google asks you not to submit multiple appeals on the same issue before you get a decision back, and in practice stacking them tends to push you to the back of the line instead of the front.

If the appeal gives you an evidence form, have your documents ready before you open it. That form has a 60 minute window, and anything you do not attach inside that hour does not get attached at all. A utility bill at the address, a signed lease, your business registration, a photo of the signage, an invoice with the address on it. Scan them first, then open the form.

What to do while you are waiting

Do not delete the profile and make a new one. That is the single worst move and it is the one people reach for around day seven out of pure frustration. A brand new listing goes to the back of the same line, and now you have two records for the same business fighting each other.

Do not change your business name, address, or category while the review is open either. An edit mid review can bounce the whole thing back to the start.

What you can do is the work that pays off the second you get verified. Get your reviews lined up so you have real ones to ask for on day one. Get your website's address block correct. Get photos shot and ready to load. Make sure your hours are right. When the green check finally lands, you want to go from verified to ranking instead of starting from scratch, and the moves that get you into the map pack are in how to actually rank on Google Maps.

Most of these clear on their own inside a week. The ones that do not are usually a video that never proved ownership or an address that does not match the rest of the internet. Fix those two and you will get through.

Common questions

How long does Google Business Profile verification take?
Google says up to 5 business days for a video review. Postcards run longer since they have to reach you in the mail. If you are past 5 business days with no movement, that is when it is fair to treat it as stuck instead of slow.

Why does my profile say pending after I already sent the video?
Usually the video was accepted but the review has not finished, or a reviewer could not see something Google needed. Missing signage, missing proof that you manage the place, a cut in the middle of the recording, or a location that does not match your address will all send it back around.

Can I record the verification video ahead of time and upload it?
No. Google wants a live recording made inside the Google Maps app on a phone, signed into the account that manages the profile. Uploads and desktop recordings are not accepted, and the video has to be one continuous take with no edits.

Should I keep submitting appeals until someone answers?
No, and it can make things worse. Google asks you not to file more than one appeal on the same issue before you get a decision, and stacked appeals tend to reset the clock. Send one, then wait for a real answer.

Is the suspension appeal tool the same as verification?
No, and people lose weeks on that mixup. The suspension appeals tool is for profiles that were suspended or restricted. It does nothing for an unverified profile that is sitting in review, so do not send your verification problem through it.
