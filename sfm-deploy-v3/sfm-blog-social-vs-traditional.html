Social Media vs Traditional Marketing: What the 2026 Numbers Actually Say

J
Jayson Hines July 20, 2026 · 7 min read

A guy who runs a shop in Perrysburg asked me last month whether he should renew his radio spot or move the money into Facebook ads. He had been running that radio ad for six years. He liked the station, he liked the rep, and he had no idea whether it was working.

That is the real question behind social media versus traditional marketing. Not which one wins in the abstract, but where a local business in Northwest Ohio should put the next dollar. So here are the actual numbers instead of a sales pitch.

The cost gap is not close

Start with what it costs to put your message in front of a thousand people. That is CPM, cost per thousand impressions, and it is the fairest way to compare channels side by side.

Social and digital ads run roughly $3 to $10 per thousand impressions in 2026. Instagram averages right around $9. Traditional media starts near $22 and climbs hard from there, with magazine placements reaching into the hundreds. Across the board, online ads cost somewhere around 70 to 85 percent less per thousand impressions than offline media.

Production is the other half of the bill. A full-page magazine ad can run $20,000 and up. Billboards land anywhere from $2,000 to $50,000 depending on the board. A direct mail campaign runs about $150 to $300 per thousand pieces. Meanwhile most small businesses running social ads spend between $850 and $2,000 a month total, creative included. If you want the full breakdown of what local marketing costs around here, I wrote about social media marketing pricing in Northwest Ohio separately.

Cheap impressions are not the real advantage

Cost is the obvious part. The bigger deal is who actually sees it.

A radio spot reaches everyone in range whether or not they will ever buy from you. A Facebook or Instagram campaign can reach homeowners in three specific zip codes, in a certain age range, who have already shown interest in what you sell. For a plumber in Findlay, that difference is enormous. You stop paying to reach people in Cleveland.

Then there is knowing whether it worked, and this is the part that gets under my skin. That shop owner had six years of radio and could not point to a single number. With digital you see impressions, clicks, calls, form fills, and cost per lead. You know what a customer costs you. You can shut off what is not working on a Tuesday afternoon.

The return numbers line up with that. Industry benchmarks for 2026 put email marketing around $42 back for every $1 spent, local SEO around $13, Google Ads around $8, and social ads around $5. Those are averages and yours will vary, but nothing in traditional media comes with that kind of tracked, repeatable figure. Social also closes at roughly double the rate of cold calling and direct mail.

Right around 72 percent of small business marketing budgets now go to digital. That shift is owners following their own results.

Where traditional still earns its keep

Now the part most agencies will not tell you, because they are selling digital.

Traditional is not dead, and in a market like ours it still has real uses.

Radio still works locally. Local ad spending is up about 3 percent in 2026, and radio and audio are leading that growth. The reason is trust. People in small towns listen to the same hosts for years, and that familiarity carries over to the businesses those hosts mention. If your customers skew older, local, and loyal, radio is not a waste of money.

Direct mail still works when it is targeted. Global direct mail spend is projected around $66 billion in 2026, and that is not nostalgia. A physical piece in the hands of the right households cuts through an inbox nobody reads. For home services hitting specific neighborhoods, it can pencil out.

Billboards still work for geography. Outdoor advertising in the US pulled in more than $10 billion. A board on the right stretch of road builds familiarity with everyone who drives it every day. That is worth something for a business that lives on local recognition.

The honest catch on all of them is the same. You usually cannot measure them well, and they take a bigger swing up front.

What I would actually do with a local budget

If you handed me a marketing budget for a Northwest Ohio business tomorrow, here is roughly how I would think about it.

Start with the free and near-free things that compound. Your Google Business Profile, your reviews, and your local search presence. Local SEO returns more per dollar than almost anything else and it keeps paying after you stop feeding it. If you have never set that up properly, start with how to rank on Google Maps.

Then put the bulk of your paid budget into targeted social and search ads. You can start small, measure everything, and scale only the campaigns that actually produce leads. If you are weighing organic content against paid, I broke that down in organic social versus paid ads.

Then, if you still have budget and an actual reason, add traditional. A radio read if your customers really listen. A mailer to specific streets. A board on a road they drive daily. Use traditional to reinforce what people are already seeing from you online.

And whatever you run, put a number on it. Use a dedicated phone number or an offer code so you can tell what came from where. The problem with that shop owner's radio ad was never radio. It was six years of not knowing.

Common questions

Is traditional marketing dead?
No. Radio, direct mail, and billboards still work, especially locally where trust and repetition matter. What changed is that they cost more per person reached and are harder to measure, so they work best as a supplement rather than your main engine.

How much should a small business spend on social media ads?
Most small businesses land between $850 and $2,000 a month including creative, though you can start well under that. Early on the point is to spend enough to gather real data, then scale the campaigns that produce leads.

Which gives better ROI, social media or traditional marketing?
On tracked return, digital wins. 2026 benchmarks put local SEO around $13 back per dollar and social ads around $5, and traditional has no comparable tracked figure. The bigger advantage is that you can measure digital and adjust it.

Should I cancel my radio ad or direct mail campaign?
Not automatically. First ask whether you can tie it to any result. Add a dedicated phone number or offer code so you can tell. If it has run for years with no way to measure it, the measurement is the problem, not the channel.

Can I do both social media and traditional marketing?
Yes, and plenty of local businesses should. The order matters though. Get your Google presence, reviews, and targeted digital working first since those are cheaper and measurable, then layer traditional on top to reinforce them.
