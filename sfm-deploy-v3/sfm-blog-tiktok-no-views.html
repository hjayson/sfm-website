Why Is My TikTok Not Getting Views?

J
Jayson Hines August 14, 2026 · 7 min read

Short answer: your TikTok isn't getting views because the small test batch of people TikTok showed it to scrolled past before the video was over. TikTok pushes every new post to a few hundred accounts first. If those people don't watch, it stops there. It's almost never a punishment and it's almost never your hashtags.

I get this question from business owners more than any other TikTok question. Somebody posts four videos, gets 180 views on each of them, and decides the platform is rigged against small accounts. It isn't. Here's what's actually going on and what you can do about it.

Why is my TikTok stuck at 0 views?

Zero is a different problem than low. If the counter says 0 and it's been sitting there for hours, that's a distribution issue and not a performance issue.

Every upload goes through an automated review before it gets shown to anybody, and that usually clears in five to thirty minutes. If something tripped a community guidelines flag, it moves to a human queue and can sit longer. Brand new accounts get throttled while TikTok decides whether you're a real person. Music you don't have rights to can get a video quietly hidden from public feeds. And sometimes the upload just glitches, so the video shows up on your profile but never actually went anywhere.

Easy way to check: pull up your profile from a friend's phone or a logged out browser. If the video isn't there, delete it and re-upload from a fresh session. If it is there and still shows 0 after a couple hours, you're waiting on review.

How does TikTok decide whether to push my video?

It shows your video to a test batch first. A few hundred accounts, some of them your followers and some of them cold. Then it watches what they do with it.

Watch time carries the most weight, specifically what percentage of the video people sat through and whether anybody looped it. After that, shares and saves. Then comments. Likes matter the least, which catches people off guard because likes are the number everybody stares at.

If that first batch watches most of the way through, TikTok hands it to a bigger batch, and it keeps doing that until people stop watching. That's the whole machine. Your follower count barely enters into it. An account with 400 followers can outrun an account with 40,000 on the same afternoon, and that should make you optimistic rather than annoyed.

Am I shadowbanned?

Probably not. Shadowbanning takes the blame for a lot of ordinary bad videos.

A real restriction has a specific shape. Every video you post drops to a fraction of your normal views, all at once, and it stays down for a week or longer. Not one post underperforming. All of them, starting the same day.

If that's what you're seeing, look at what changed right before it. Reposted content with somebody else's watermark on it, claims about health or money that got flagged, comment behavior that looked automated, or an actual strike sitting in your account inbox. Go to Settings, then Account, and see if there's a warning in there. Clean it up, post original stuff for a couple weeks, and it usually resets on its own.

If your views have always been low, nothing is restricted. You've got a content problem, and that one is way easier to fix.

How do I actually fix low views?

Start with the first second. Not the first three seconds, the first one. Most people lose the viewer before the opening sentence is even finished. If you're a roofer, don't open with "hey guys, today I want to talk about." Open with the hail damage.

Then cut the video shorter than feels comfortable. A 22 second video somebody watches twice will beat a 90 second video they bail on at second 12, because TikTok reads the percentage. Same content, shorter runtime, better math.

Say something specific enough to be worth watching. Vague advice gets ignored on every platform now. "Post consistently" is nothing. "We charge $450 for this and here's exactly why" gets watched all the way through and argued about in the comments, and people arguing in your comments is free distribution.

Post four or five times a week for a solid month before you judge any of it. One video tells you nothing at all. TikTok is a testing platform and you can't test anything with a sample size of three.

And go read your analytics for average watch time instead of views. Views are the result. Watch time is the part you can actually move. If people are dropping off at second four, the hook is broken. If they're making it to second 20 of a 30 second clip, you're close and you just need more reps. When I can't figure out what to even make, I go back to a list of social media content ideas for local businesses and pick one, and most of those work on TikTok as easily as they do on Facebook.

Do posting times and hashtags matter?

Barely, on both counts.

Hashtags stopped being a discovery tool years ago. TikTok reads your caption, your on-screen text, and your spoken audio to figure out what a video is about. Two or three relevant tags are plenty. Thirty of them eats caption space you could've used for a hook.

Posting time matters a little, mostly for that first hour of engagement. If your customers are on their phones at 7pm, post around 7pm. But a good video at a bad time still travels and a bad video at the perfect time still dies. Fix the video first, then worry about the clock.

Should a local business even bother with TikTok?

Depends entirely on who buys from you.

If you sell to homeowners in their 30s and 40s, they're on there, and the platform has skewed older every year since 2020. Med spas, restaurants, salons, home services, and anything with a real before-and-after tend to do well. If your customer is a 60 year old plant manager buying industrial equipment, spend your time somewhere else and don't feel bad about it.

What I like about TikTok for a small business here in Northwest Ohio is that the platform doesn't care how big you are. It cares whether the video held attention. That's a fairer fight than most channels hand you. Figuring out which platforms your business should actually be on, and what to put on them, is the first thing we work out on the social media management side before anybody points a camera at anything.

Common questions

Why did my TikTok views suddenly drop?
Look at whether the drop hit every video at once or just the newer ones. If every post fell off on the same day and stayed down for a week, check your account inbox for a guideline warning. If only the recent ones are soft, your content changed and the hooks got weaker.

How many views should a new TikTok account get?
A few hundred per video is normal for the first month. TikTok tests new accounts carefully. What you want to watch is whether your average watch time is climbing week over week, because that is the number that eventually gets you into bigger test batches.

Should I delete a TikTok that flopped?
No. Deleting does not give you a fresh start and posting then deleting repeatedly can look like spam behavior. Leave it up and make the next one better.

Does reposting my TikTok to Instagram Reels hurt me?
Cross posting is fine. Reposting a file that still has the TikTok watermark on it will get throttled on Reels, and the reverse is true too. Export a clean version without the watermark and upload that to each platform.

How long before TikTok starts pushing my videos?
Give it four to six weeks of posting four or five times a week. That is enough volume for the algorithm to figure out who your videos belong in front of. Judging TikTok off five posts is like judging a radio ad off one morning.
