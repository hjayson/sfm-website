Why Is My TikTok Video Blurry?

J
Jayson HinesOctober 18, 2026 · 5 min read

When a TikTok video looks blurry after you post it, check the original clip, export, upload path, and playback connection before you record it again. A soft source or a file compressed by an editing or messaging app can lose detail before TikTok gets it. Start with a quick comparison.

How can I tell whether the blur is in my file or on TikTok?

Play the original video from your camera roll, then open the posted version on a steady Wi-Fi connection. Check it again on another phone or ask someone nearby to look. If the original is already fuzzy, fix the recording. If the original is sharp but the post is soft on more than one device, check your export and upload steps.

Give the post a little time to finish processing before making a decision. A weak connection can also make playback look rough, so avoid judging the upload while your phone is switching between Wi-Fi and mobile data.

Could the camera or lighting be making the video soft?

Wipe the lens and tap the subject on screen to focus before recording. More light usually helps a phone capture a cleaner image, especially indoors. Move closer instead of relying on digital zoom. If the camera is struggling to focus, a sharp export will not fix the footage.

Record a short test and watch it before you start the full take. That small check can save you from editing a whole clip that was out of focus from the start.

Did editing or sending the file reduce its quality?

Keep the original camera file and export directly from your editing app. Re-saving a video several times, downloading it from a social app, or sending it through a service that compresses attachments can soften it. Move the original file with a method that preserves its quality when you can.

Use a vertical frame that fills the phone screen. TikTok for Business recommends a 9:16 frame and resolution of at least 720p in its creative guidance for TikTok ads. That is a practical baseline for clear footage, though individual posts can still look different across connections and devices.

Which TikTok upload settings should I check?

On the posting screen, look for an upload in HD or higher quality option if your app offers it. The exact controls can vary. Check your data settings too. Data Saver may affect how video plays on your phone, so compare playback with it off and on Wi-Fi before you re-edit the clip.

Also update TikTok if the app has been acting oddly, then try one clean upload from the original export. Changing several settings at once makes it harder to tell what helped.

Should I delete and upload the video again?

Wait until you have compared the post with the source file on a good connection. If the uploaded copy stays visibly soft, correct the export or recording first, then consider posting the improved version. Re-uploading the same compressed file usually gives you the same result.

Common questions

Why is my TikTok video blurry after I upload it?
The source file may be soft, the export or transfer may have compressed it, or playback may be using a lower quality stream. Compare the posted video with the original file and check it on a strong connection before changing anything.

What resolution should I use for a TikTok video?
Start with a clear vertical file. TikTok for Business recommends 720p or higher and a full-screen 9:16 frame for ad creative. That is useful production guidance, though delivery can still vary by device, connection, and platform processing.

Does the Upload HD setting fix blurry videos?
It can help when the option is available, but it cannot restore detail that was lost in recording, editing, or file transfer. Check the setting on the posting screen and keep a copy of the original export.

Why does my TikTok video look blurry only on my phone?
Playback can change with connection quality, data settings, or the screen you are using. Check the post on Wi-Fi and on another device before assuming the uploaded file itself is damaged.

Should I delete and repost a blurry TikTok video?
Not right away. First compare it with the original and check it again on another connection. If the uploaded copy is still clearly soft, correct the source or export and then decide whether a new upload is worthwhile.
