The Best Time to Post on Facebook and Instagram in 2026 (For Local Businesses)

J
Jayson Hines August 10, 2026 · 6 min read

Short answer, then the honest one. For most local businesses the safe windows to post on Facebook and Instagram are weekday mornings around 7 to 9am, again around lunch, and in the evening after 7pm, with the middle of the week usually beating Monday and Friday. That is the generic answer you will find in every article, and it is fine to start there.

But if you stop at a generic chart you are missing the whole point. The best time to post is whenever your followers are actually on their phones, and yours are not the internet average. You can find your real number in about two minutes, and I will show you how below.

Why there is no universal "best time"

Every one of those "post at 11:03am Tuesday" articles is averaging millions of accounts across every industry and country. Your bakery in Perrysburg has nothing in common with a fitness page in Los Angeles or a software brand in London. When a study spits out one magic time, that is the average of a giant pile of accounts, and your audience is not that pile.

I have clients whose best engagement is at 6am because their customers are contractors already up and moving. I have others who peak at 9pm once the kids are down. A blog post can't know that about your people. Your own account can.

How to find your real best time in two minutes

On Instagram, switch to a professional account if you have not already, then open Insights, tap Total Followers, and scroll to Most Active Times. It shows you the hours and days your followers are actually online. On Facebook, open Meta Business Suite, go to Insights, and look at the "when your followers are online" view.

Post around those peaks for two weeks, watch which posts land, and adjust. That one check beats every generic timing chart on the internet, because it is about your customers instead of everyone's.

What to do before you have your own data

If your account is brand new and there is not enough data yet, start with the safe windows. Weekday mornings while people scroll before work, the lunch stretch, and the evening after dinner. Mid-week is usually your best bet. Weekends go quiet for a lot of local businesses, though food, events, and anything people do on their days off can buck that.

Facebook and Instagram are not the same

Facebook skews a little older and gets more midday and lunchtime activity. Instagram tends to peak in the morning and again at night, and it is almost entirely a phone habit. One thing that trips people up: Reels don't really care when you post. The apps keep showing a good Reel for days after it goes up, so for video the exact minute matters far less than whether the video is any good.

The thing that matters more than timing

A great post at an average time beats a boring post at the perfect time, every single day. The algorithm keeps pushing content people stop and watch, and it buries content they scroll past, no matter what the clock said when you hit publish.

So if you are going to obsess over anything, obsess over the first second of the post and how consistently you show up. If your content isn't getting watched, timing is not your problem, and that is what our content creation work is built around. The done-for-you version of all of this is our social media management, where posting at the right time for your audience is just part of the job.

What I actually tell Northwest Ohio business owners

Forget the national charts and think about your customer's real day. A home services company's customers scroll at night after work, so that is when you show up. A coffee shop wants the morning. A restaurant wants late afternoon, right when people start thinking about dinner. Post when your people are making the decision you want to be part of, not when a chart from a company in another country says to.

And whatever windows you land on, keep a rhythm. Posting on a couple of set days each week at roughly the same time trains both the algorithm and your audience to expect you. A decent time you hit consistently will always beat a perfect time you only hit once. For more on that, here are some content ideas you can actually keep up with.

Common questions

What is the best time to post on Instagram in 2026?
Generally weekday mornings and evenings, with mid-week the strongest. But the real answer is in your own Instagram Insights under Most Active Times, which shows exactly when your followers are online. Post around those peaks.

What is the best time to post on Facebook?
Facebook tends to get the most activity midday and early afternoon on weekdays, since it skews a bit older and sees more lunchtime and desktop use. Check Meta Business Suite Insights to see when your specific followers are online.

Does the time you post matter for Reels?
Much less than for a normal post. Reels keep getting surfaced for days after you publish, so a good Reel will find an audience whenever you post it. Put your energy into the video being worth watching.

How often should a local business post?
Consistency matters more than volume. A few solid posts a week you can actually keep up beats a daily grind you burn out on in a month. Going dark for weeks is what actually hurts a local page.

Is it better to post at the same time every day?
A steady rhythm helps. It trains your audience to expect you and gives the algorithm a pattern. The exact minute matters less than showing up on a schedule you can keep.
