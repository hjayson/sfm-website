02 · TRY THIS TODAY

Write a fit statement a customer can use

Give this 20 minutes. Start with one service or product, especially one that people often compare with another choice.

  1. Write down two real situations you handle well. Use customer questions or completed work as your evidence. Leave names and private details out.
  2. Name one reasonable alternative. It could be doing the task yourself, choosing a basic package, or hiring a different kind of provider. Say when that option might be enough.
  3. Explain when your approach earns its place. Describe a specific step, deliverable, or service boundary you can verify. Keep prices and outcome claims out unless you have current proof.
  4. Turn those notes into three short lines: 'Good fit if...', 'Consider another option if...', and 'Here's what happens next.' Put them near the relevant service description or test them as one social post. Ask a colleague to read it for accuracy before publishing.

For a local bookkeeper: 'Good fit if you need someone to reconcile accounts each month and explain what the numbers mean. If you have just a few transactions and enjoy keeping your own records, software alone may be enough. If you'd like help, we'll start by reviewing your current books and scope the work.' Only use that wording if the service really works that way.

03 · JAYSON'S TAKE

A good fit statement can say no.

Small businesses often feel pressure to make every visitor sound like the perfect customer. That usually produces copy so broad it could sit on any competitor's site. The more useful move is to tell people when your approach makes sense. You can be specific without claiming you're the only one who does the work well.

I'd start with a question you hear before a sale: 'Do I need the full service?' or 'Could I handle this myself?' Answer it fairly. Name the case where the simpler option works, then show the extra work you do when it doesn't. That gives a customer a way to decide and gives your team a clearer starting point for the next conversation. If AI helps draft the language, check it against the service you actually deliver.
