Why Is My Facebook Reel Not Getting Views?

J
Jayson Hines August 25, 2026 · 7 min read

Short answer: on Facebook a Reel gets shown to a small test group first, and if that group swipes past it in the first couple of seconds, the Reel stops right there. Your follower count barely matters. Views are earned per video, and most of what a Reel is going to do gets decided in the first hour after you hit post.

I get this question from local business owners about once a week. They posted five Reels, the first one did fine, and the next four did almost nothing. No warning, no takedown, the reach just fell off a cliff. So here is what is actually happening and what to go check.

How does Facebook decide who sees my Reel?

Facebook treats Reels as recommendations, not as posts to your followers. When you publish, it shows the video to a small slice of people, some who follow you and plenty who do not. Then it watches what they do. Watch time is the big one. Did people stay past the first couple of seconds, and did enough of them make it all the way through.

If that first group keeps watching, Facebook widens the circle. If they swipe, it stops. That is how a page with a few thousand followers can post a Reel and barely get seen. Followers only tell Facebook where to start looking. They do not guarantee anybody watches.

Why did my Reels get views before and not now?

Usually one of two things. Either the content shifted, or the page went quiet for a stretch and then came back.

The content one is more common. Somebody has a Reel take off, so they make ten more exactly like it, and the people who already watched the first one have no reason to watch the rest. Facebook reads that as interest dropping and slows the whole page down. Repeating a format is fine. Repeating the same video with a new hat on is not.

The gap one trips up a lot of small businesses. You post hard for two weeks, get busy, go dark for a month, then come back and everything flops. That feels like a punishment. Really the system is just re-learning who your videos are for, and that takes a couple weeks of consistent posting to sort out.

Does a TikTok watermark really hurt your Facebook Reel?

Yes, and this one is the fastest thing on the list to fix. Meta has been open about demoting recycled video from other apps, and a visible watermark is the easiest possible thing for it to spot. Same goes for the TikTok caption font and that end screen with the username on it.

If you are pulling videos out of TikTok with a downloader and dumping them onto your Facebook page, that is very likely your entire problem. Export the clean file out of your editor instead, or save it out of TikTok drafts before the app stamps it. Takes an extra minute. Worth it. If TikTok is the side giving you trouble, I went through that separately in why your TikTok videos are not getting views.

What should the first three seconds do?

Show the thing. That is it.

Most business Reels open with a logo animation, or a wide shot of the building, or somebody saying "hey guys, welcome back," or fifteen seconds of a slow drone pass over a parking lot. All of those are a thumb swipe. The person scrolling has no relationship with you and zero patience.

Open on the payoff or the problem. A roofer opens on the hole in the decking. A restaurant opens on the cheese pull. A dentist opens on the before photo. A lawn guy opens on the finished yard. If you are talking to camera, your first sentence should be the actual point, not the wind-up to the point. Move the intro to the middle if you need it at all.

Does posting time matter for Facebook Reels?

Less than people think, and a lot less than it matters for a regular photo post. Reels get resurfaced for days and sometimes weeks, so a good one posted at a bad hour still finds people. A bad one posted at the perfect hour still dies.

That said, the first hour is when the test group forms, so you do want to post when your people are awake and scrolling. For most local businesses around Northwest Ohio that lands early morning or the 7pm to 10pm window. I broke the full version of that down in the best time to post on social media.

Am I shadowbanned, or is the Reel just not good?

Nine times out of ten it is the video. Real restrictions come with a notice attached. Open Meta Business Suite, check your Support Inbox, and look at the Page Quality tab. If nothing is sitting in there, nobody flagged you.

Two things do quietly hurt you without being a penalty. First is music. Business pages do not get the full consumer music library, so a track can end up muted in certain places and you would never know unless you checked from another account. Second is text placement. If your captions sit in the bottom third of the frame, the Facebook interface covers them, and people who cannot read the video swipe off it.

If you are hitting the same wall on the other app, here is why Instagram Reels get no views, and if it is your regular posts dying too, that is a different problem I covered in why Facebook reach is so low.

What would I actually do for the next 30 days?

Post four or five Reels a week, uploaded straight to Facebook, no watermark, shot vertical and filling the whole frame. Open every single one on the actual subject. Keep most of them short and only let one run long when there is a real reason to.

Then start looking at retention instead of views. Business Suite shows you a drop-off curve for each Reel. If most people leave in the first three seconds, your opener is the problem. If they get to the middle and quit, your pacing is. Views are the score at the end. Retention tells you why you got it.

And quit deleting the ones that flop. Deleting does not reset anything. It just throws away a data point Facebook was using to figure out who your stuff is for.

Common questions

How long does it take for a Facebook Reel to start getting views?
The first hour tells you most of it, but Reels keep getting resurfaced for days and sometimes weeks after you post. Don't judge one ten minutes in, and don't assume a slow start means it's dead.

Do hashtags help Facebook Reels?
Barely. A couple of relevant ones won't hurt you, but Facebook is reading the video itself and your caption text far more than your hashtag list. Time spent picking hashtags is better spent on the first three seconds.

Should I delete a Reel that got no views?
No. Deleting doesn't reset your reach or clear anything out. You just lose the data. Leave it up and make the next one better.

Can I post the same Reel to Facebook and Instagram?
Yes, as long as the file is clean with no watermark from either app. Upload it separately to each one instead of using the cross-post toggle, since the shared version tends to get treated as a copy.

How many Reels a week should a small business post?
Four or five is a good target if you can hold the quality there. Staying consistent matters more than volume, and a long gap costs you more reach than a slow week does.
