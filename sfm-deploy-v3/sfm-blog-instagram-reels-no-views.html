Why Are My Instagram Reels Not Getting Views?

J
Jayson Hines August 20, 2026 · 7 min read

Short answer: your Reel stalled because not enough people watched past the opening. Instagram shows every new Reel to a small test group first. If those folks swipe away in the first couple of seconds, the app quietly stops handing it to anybody else, and your view count parks itself around 200 and sits there.

Almost everybody assumes shadowban. In my experience it's almost always the opening two seconds.

Why did my Reels views suddenly drop?

A sudden drop usually traces back to something that changed on your end rather than a punishment from Instagram. You went quiet for two weeks. Or you started posting a different kind of video than the one your audience followed you for.

I watched a contractor here in Wood County do exactly that. He built his following on job site walkthroughs, muddy boots, showing what a bad roof deck looks like underneath. Real stuff. Then he got some advice about "branding" and started posting quote graphics with music over them. Views fell off a cliff inside of a month. Instagram had him sorted into a lane and he walked out of it.

Worth saying: if every format you post is down, not just Reels, that's a different problem and I covered it separately in why your Instagram reach is suddenly dropping. This post is about Reels specifically.

The other common one is that you got comfortable. Your first videos were rough and honest and people watched them all the way through. Then you got a little polished. A little slower to start. The good part drifted into the second half of the video where nobody is still watching.

Does Instagram really punish you for posting TikToks?

Yes, and this catches a lot of people. If you export a video from TikTok with the watermark still stamped on it and drop it into Reels, Instagram can see that watermark. It has been pretty open about wanting original video made for Instagram instead of leftovers from a competitor.

The fix is easy. Save the video without the watermark before you post it, or just upload the original file off your phone. Same video, much better distribution.

What settings are quietly capping your views?

There's a handful of toggles buried in Instagram that most business accounts never touch, and a couple of them cost you reach.

Make sure your account isn't set to private. Sounds obvious. I've sat across the table from two business owners this year who had it on and had no idea. Check that the option allowing your public photos and videos to be recommended hasn't been switched off. Turn captions on, because a lot of people watch with the sound down and captions keep them watching longer. If there's a setting to let your Reel show up in search, leave it on.

One more worth knowing. If you used a song with odd licensing, Instagram can mute or limit the Reel in certain places. So if a video did fine for a day and then flatlined, go check whether the audio got pulled.

Am I shadowbanned, or is my content just not landing?

Real restrictions exist, and they come with a notice. Open Settings, then Account Status. If Instagram has limited your account or flagged something you posted, it says so right there. No notice usually means nothing is wrong with your account.

What most people call a shadowban is a Reel that simply didn't earn distribution. That stings, but it's fixable, which an actual ban isn't.

Here's a quick gut check. Pull up your insights on the last ten Reels and look at average watch time instead of views. If people are bailing at two seconds, your opening is the problem and no amount of hashtag strategy is going to save it.

How do I actually get more views on Reels?

Start where the problem is. The first two seconds have to give somebody a reason to stay. Not a logo animation. Not "hey guys." Not you reaching up to adjust the camera. Start in the middle of the sentence if you have to. Show the mess before you show the fix.

Then quit watching likes. Watch saves and shares. A like costs somebody nothing and means about that much. When a person sends your video to their spouse or saves it for later, that's the signal Instagram reads as worth showing to more people. So make things people want to save. The cost breakdown nobody posts. The mistake you watched a homeowner make last week. The thing your competitors won't say out loud.

Post steadily, too. Not ten times a day. Just often enough that the algorithm has recent information about who you are and who likes you. Three or four times a week beats a burst of twelve videos followed by a month of silence.

And go answer your comments in the first hour after you post. Every reply is another signal that the thing is alive.

If you run a local business and this is starting to sound like a second job stacked on top of the one you already have, that's a fair reaction. It's most of what we handle for clients through our social media management services, and it's the reason the work gets done consistently instead of whenever somebody remembers.

How long should I wait before calling a Reel dead?

Give it 48 hours before you decide anything. Reels have a much longer tail than a regular feed post. I've watched videos sit at 300 views for two days and then jump to 40,000 because one person with reach shared it into their stories.

Don't delete a Reel six hours in because it's off to a slow start. Deleting also throws out whatever Instagram had figured out about who the video was for, and you don't get that back.

If it's still flat after two days, leave it up and move on. Then go pull up the ones that did work and ask yourself what those openings had that the dead one didn't. That answer is worth more than any trick.

Common questions

Why do my Instagram Reels get stuck at 200 views?
That number is roughly the size of the test group Instagram shows a new Reel to first. If those viewers swipe away early, the app stops distributing it and the count freezes. It almost always means weak watch time in the opening seconds, not a penalty on your account.

Should I delete a Reel that didn't get views?
No. Reels have a long tail and can pick up days later if somebody shares them. Deleting also throws away the data Instagram gathered about who the video suited. Leave it up, and study the openings of the Reels that did work instead.

Do hashtags still help Reels get views?
A little, but far less than people think. Instagram leans on watch time, saves, shares, and what your account normally posts about. Three or four relevant hashtags are fine. Thirty of them will not rescue a video nobody finishes.

Does posting at the right time actually matter for Reels?
It matters less for Reels than for feed posts, because Reels keep getting distributed for days rather than hours. Posting when your audience is awake helps that first test group respond faster, but a good hook beats good timing every time.

How many Reels should I post a week to grow?
Three or four is a realistic target for most local businesses, and consistency beats volume. A steady few per week gives the algorithm recent data on you. Twelve videos in one weekend followed by a month of nothing does not.
