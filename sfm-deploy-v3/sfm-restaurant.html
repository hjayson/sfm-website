HOW WE WORK WITH RESTAURANTS

Aimed at the shifts
that actually need help.

Campaigns pointed at your soft shifts

There is no reason to spend money making Saturday busier. We build around the nights that are burning labor with nothing to show for it, with a specific offer, a specific date, and a radius that reflects how far people will genuinely drive on a Tuesday in February.

Pushing orders to your door instead of the app

Every order that comes through you instead of a third party keeps the commission in your pocket, and on the margins independents run, that difference is often the whole profit on the ticket. Direct ordering, call-ahead, and pickup get the promotion, not the delivery platforms taking a cut of every plate.

The unglamorous hygiene that decides first visits

Current photos, accurate hours, a menu with prices that someone can actually find on a phone, and recent reviews. When you 86 something regularly or change hours seasonally, that needs to be reflected where people are looking. This is low-glamour work that quietly determines whether a stranger picks you.

Catering and private events as their own line

Office parties, graduations, and holiday orders run on a completely different lead time than dine-in, and they usually carry better margins than a table that turns twice. Most independent places underwork this and leave real revenue sitting there. It deserves its own campaigns rather than an occasional mention.

WHY RESTAURANTS ARE DIFFERENT

Marketing a restaurant
is a math problem, not a food photo.

Anyone can shoot a nice plate. The question is whether it moved covers on the shift where you actually needed them, because your fixed costs do not care how good Saturday was.

A dead Tuesday costs you more than a packed Saturday makes

Saturday volume is already baked into your model. Tuesday is the one that hurts, because rent, insurance, and salaried management do not flex down when only forty covers walk in and you are still fully staffed. Marketing that only lifts your busiest shift is not really helping. The job is filling the shifts that are burning labor with nothing to absorb it.

Third-party delivery can turn a sale into a loss

Commissions commonly run fifteen to thirty percent, and once the added fees and promos land, operators routinely describe the effective bite as closer to a third of that order. On margins as thin as independent restaurants run, a delivery order can clear almost nothing. That is why the work should push people toward your own door, your own phone, and your own catering, not toward the app.

January and the college calendar are both real

The industry goes quiet in January and February everywhere, and around here lake-effect weather keeps regulars home on top of it. If you are anywhere near BGSU or UT, move-out hollows out your lunch and happy-hour traffic in a way that has nothing to do with your food. Patio season is the window that makes it back, and holiday catering is a separate revenue line most places underwork.

A page that has not posted since 2023 reads as closed

Before someone drives over, they check. They look for recent photos, recent reviews, an accurate menu, and hours that match reality. A stale page tells them you might not be open, and the safest choice is the place that posted yesterday. Nothing kills a first visit faster than driving over to a dark dining room because the hours were wrong.
