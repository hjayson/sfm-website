Why Does My Google Business Profile Say Permanently Closed?

J
Jayson Hines September 24, 2026 · 7 min read

Short answer: your profile says permanently closed because somebody or something told Google you closed, and Google believed it. Usually that's a user submitted "Suggest an edit" that got auto accepted, an old data feed or a duplicate listing still reporting the closure, a move that Google read as a shutdown, or you clicked the closed setting yourself at some point and forgot. The fix is to open your Business Profile, mark the business as open, and then hunt down whatever else is still telling Google otherwise.

This one costs real money while it's live. A closed label kills your map pack visibility, greys out your listing, and people who were about to call you just don't. I've had business owners in Perrysburg and Toledo find out from a customer, not from Google.

Why did Google mark my business permanently closed?

There's no notification for this most of the time, which is why it feels random. But it's almost always one of a handful of causes.

The big one is Suggest an edit. Anybody with a Google account can pull up your listing on Maps, tap Suggest an edit, and report you as closed. They don't have to contact you and they don't have to prove anything. If the person has a Local Guide history, or if a few different accounts report the same thing, Google's system can accept it automatically. No human ever looks at it.

Second cause is bad data coming in from somewhere else. Google pulls signals from third party sources, aggregators, and other listings about your business. If an old directory page or a stale data feed still says you shut down in 2023, that can push the status back even after you fix it.

Third is a duplicate listing. Really common with businesses that moved or changed names. The duplicate gets marked closed, and then the closed status bleeds onto what customers see. If you've got two versions of yourself on Maps, sort that out first, and I wrote a whole walkthrough on what to do about a duplicate Google Business Profile.

Fourth is you, or whoever had the login before you. During covid a lot of owners set themselves to temporarily closed and never cleared it. Others hit the wrong option while editing. Happens more than people admit.

And the last one is a move. You changed addresses, Google saw the old address go quiet, and instead of following you it flagged the old spot as closed.

Can a competitor mark my business as closed?

Yes. That's the uncomfortable part. The Suggest an edit feature is open to everyone, and it gets used as a weapon in competitive local markets. Contractors, tow companies, locksmiths, dispensaries, anything with a crowded map pack, this stuff happens.

There's also an older scam where somebody spams closure edits on a listing and then contacts the owner offering to "fix" it for a fee. If you get that call, don't pay it. You can fix it yourself in about two minutes.

What you can't do is find out who did it. Google doesn't tell you. So don't burn a week trying to prove it was the guy across town. Just get the status corrected and then watch the listing, because whoever did it once will usually try again.

How do I change permanently closed back to open?

Do it in this order.

1: Sign in to the Google account that manages the listing and search your business name on Google, or go to Google Business Profile Manager. 2: Find the business status setting, which is usually under the info or edit profile area, and choose the option to mark it as open or remove the closed label. 3: Save it. 4: While you're in there, verify your hours, phone number, address, website, and primary category are all correct, since Google weighs those when it decides whether to trust the reopen. 5: Post something. A photo, an update, anything that shows the profile is active. 6: Check the listing on a different device or in an incognito window a few hours later.

If the setting is greyed out, locked, or you can't find the business status control at all, that's usually a sign you're either not on the right account or the listing has a bigger problem behind it. A profile that's actually suspended behaves differently than one that's just mislabeled, and the fix path is different too, so read what to do when your Google Business Profile gets suspended before you start filing appeals.

When the reopen doesn't stick, go to Google Business Profile support and open a case. Have proof ready. A photo of the storefront with signage, a recent utility bill, a business license, something with the address on it. Support moves a lot faster when you hand them evidence in the first message instead of arguing about it for three rounds.

What if I moved instead of closing?

Don't let the old address sit there marked closed, and don't create a fresh listing for the new address either. Both of those cost you.

Update the address on the existing profile so it moves with you. That keeps your reviews, your photos, and everything else that makes the listing rank in the first place. Start over from scratch and you're back at zero with no reviews.

Google does have a moved status that's different from permanently closed, and it handles the handoff better. If you're in that situation, say so plainly when you contact support instead of just asking them to mark you open.

How long does it take for Google to fix it?

Sometimes a few hours. Usually a couple days. Occasionally a week or more if it needs a human at Google to sign off, or if the closed signal is coming from an outside source that keeps re-feeding it.

What you shouldn't do is keep toggling the setting back and forth every few hours because it hasn't updated yet. That looks unstable and doesn't speed anything up. Set it, post some activity, and check once a day.

If it flips back to closed on its own after you fixed it, something outside your profile is still reporting the closure. That's when you start checking other directories, your old duplicate, Apple Maps, Yelp, Bing, and any aggregator that's got you listed. Clean those up or the same thing keeps happening.

What do I do while it's still showing closed?

You've got customers seeing a closed sign right now, so plug the hole while the fix works its way through.

Put a line on your homepage and your social profiles saying you're open, with your hours. Ask a few recent customers to leave a review, because activity on the listing helps and reviews are the loudest signal you've got. If you run ads, send that traffic straight to your site instead of the profile for a few days. And answer the phone, because the people who still call anyway are the ones worth keeping.

The bigger lesson is that your Google Business Profile is the most valuable thing you don't own. One stranger with a Google account can take it down for a week. Somebody needs to be checking it regularly, keeping the info current, and posting to it so the listing looks alive. And if the closed label is gone but you're still nowhere in the map pack, that's a separate problem, start with how to actually rank on Google Maps.

Common questions

Why does Google say my business is permanently closed when it isn't?
Most of the time a user submitted Suggest an edit got accepted automatically, or an old data source or duplicate listing is still reporting the closure. It can also happen after a move, or because the closed setting was switched on in the profile and never turned back off.

How do I remove the permanently closed label from my Google listing?
Sign in to the Google account that manages the profile, open the business status setting, and mark the business as open. Then confirm your hours, address, phone, website, and category are right, and post a photo or update so the profile shows activity.

Can a competitor get my Google Business Profile marked closed?
Yes. Suggest an edit is open to any Google user and gets abused in competitive local markets. Google will not tell you who submitted it, so fix the status and keep watching the listing instead of trying to identify the person.

How long does it take Google to mark my business open again?
Often a few hours, commonly a couple of days, and sometimes a week or more if a human review is needed or an outside data source keeps re-reporting the closure. Avoid toggling the setting repeatedly while you wait.

Should I create a new listing if my old one says permanently closed?
No. A new listing starts with no reviews, no photos, and no history, which is most of what makes a profile rank. Recover or update the existing profile instead, and if you moved, change the address on the current listing rather than starting over.
