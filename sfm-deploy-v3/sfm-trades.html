HOW WE WORK WITH CONTRACTORS

Built around your season,
not a generic content calendar.

We run the calendar against your actual season

Exterior work carries you from roughly April through November, so the audience for it has to be built in late winter, not in May when you are already booked solid. Then the message pivots to interiors, basements, and kitchens before the weather turns. The point is to walk into spring with people already waiting instead of restarting from nothing every year.

Proof content, because that is what closes the trust gap

Finished jobs, work in progress, your registration and certificate of insurance, the crew that will actually be on site. Walkthrough footage and punch list follow-through are unglamorous and they answer the exact question a nervous homeowner is asking. This is the content that separates you from the guy who took a deposit and vanished.

Reviews collected at the moment they are easiest to get

The best time to ask is right after the final walkthrough, when the work is fresh and the homeowner is happy. Most contractors mean to ask and never do. We build that request into the end of the job so your review count climbs steadily instead of in occasional bursts.

Filtering for jobs you actually want

Not every call is a good call. Content that is plain about the kind of work you take, roughly what it costs, and how your draw schedule works will lose you some tire-kickers, which is the point. You spend less time writing bids for people who were never going to hire you.

WHY TRADES ARE DIFFERENT

Marketing a contracting business
is not like marketing a store.

You are asking a homeowner to hand over a deposit and let your crew into their house. That is a bigger ask than almost any other business makes, and it changes what your marketing has to do.

The deposit-and-disappear story is in every homeowner's head

Everyone knows someone who paid a contractor up front and never saw them again. It is the single loudest objection you are fighting, and you are fighting it before you ever pick up the phone. Showing your registration, your certificate of insurance, your crew, and finished work in the feed does more to close that gap than any headline. Lucas County homeowners increasingly ask to see it, and Toledo's remodeling license already requires a tested contractor with documented experience.

Lead services sold your name to four other guys

Contractors on the trade forums say the same thing over and over: you pay per lead, the same job goes out to several companies at once, and half the numbers are bad with no refund. You end up racing three competitors to the phone on a job you already paid for. Building your own audience means the homeowner asks for you by name instead of filling out a form that gets resold.

Winter is a cliff, not a slow patch

Around here the exterior season runs roughly April through November, then December through February goes quiet. The contractors who stay busy are the ones who built the audience in September and pivoted the message to interior work, basements, and kitchens before the phone stopped. Marketing that only runs when you are already busy is backwards.

More leads will not fix a job that bleeds on change orders

Margins in construction are thinner than most people outside the trade assume, and the money usually leaks in the middle of the job, not at the bid. Unsigned change orders, scope creep, callbacks, and a sub who no-shows and pushes everyone behind him. The goal is not more calls. It is better-fit calls from homeowners who already understand what you charge and why.
