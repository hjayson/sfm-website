Why Can't I Go Live on My Facebook Page?

J
Jayson Hines September 3, 2026 · 7 min read

Short answer: Facebook has eligibility rules for live video, and most people who can't go live are failing one of them without knowing it. Your account has to be at least 60 days old, and Pages and profiles in professional mode need at least 100 followers. If you clear both of those and the Live button still isn't there, it's almost always a permissions problem on the Page or a restriction left over from something Facebook flagged on your account.

Facebook is bad about telling you which one it is. You just get a vague "you can't go live yet" message, or the option is missing from the composer entirely. So let's go through them in the order I'd actually check them.

What are the requirements to go live on Facebook?

Two hard rules cover most of the errors.

Your Facebook account has to be at least 60 days old. Not the Page, the personal account behind it. If you made a brand new profile last week to run your business Page, you're waiting.

And you need 100 followers on the Page or on a profile that's in professional mode. Facebook added that one in June 2024 and it caught a lot of small businesses off guard, because plenty of local Pages sit at 40 or 60 followers for years and nobody cared until they went to stream something. Personal profiles that aren't using professional mode don't have the follower requirement, but they still have the 60 day rule.

If you're sitting under 100, that's your project for the next couple weeks. Invite your existing customers, invite friends, post something people actually want to share. It's a low bar and you'll clear it faster than you think.

Do I have the right access on the Page?

This is the one that burns agencies and office managers. You can be listed on a Page and still not have permission to create content, which means no Live button.

Facebook needs you to have either Facebook access with content permissions turned on, or task access that includes creating content. When Meta moved everybody to the new Pages experience, a bunch of old admin and editor roles got translated over in ways that didn't match what people expected. Somebody who used to be an editor might now have partial access that lets them reply to messages and nothing else.

Go into Page settings, look at Page access, and check what you actually have next to your name. If you're not the owner, the owner has to fix it. I wrote up the whole permissions mess in how to figure out who's actually the admin of your Facebook page, and it's worth reading if more than one person has ever touched the account.

Also worth ruling out: are you even in the right account? If you manage four Pages and you're posting as your personal profile, the Live option looks different and you may be staring at the wrong composer.

Is my account restricted?

If you've had content pulled for community standards, or you got hit with a copyright claim on a video, Facebook can quietly block live streaming for a stretch of time. It's a separate penalty from having a post removed. Nothing announces it. The Live option just stops working.

Check your Page Quality or Account Status page. It's under Professional dashboard, and it'll list any violations and whether you have restrictions on your account right now. If there's a restriction with an end date on it, you're waiting it out, and appealing is worth a shot if you think the original strike was wrong.

Music is the sneaky one here. Streaming a live video with a radio playing in the background, or with a popular song running through your speakers, gets flagged by Facebook's audio matching more often than people expect. That's a fast way to end up restricted from the exact feature you were trying to use.

Why does Facebook Live keep failing on my phone?

When the requirements are all met and it still won't start, it usually comes down to something boring.

Old app version. Facebook rolls live features out through app updates and an app you haven't updated since spring can be missing the option entirely. Update it, then force close and reopen.

Camera and microphone permissions. Check your phone settings and make sure Facebook has access to both. If you denied one at some point, live video won't start and the error you get won't say why.

Connection. Live needs steady upload speed, not just download. A phone showing full bars on a weak tower or a cafe wifi that's fine for scrolling will still drop a stream. If you're streaming from inside a building with bad reception, plan for it and go outside or use a hotspot.

And if you're streaming through OBS, StreamYard, Restream, or anything else with a stream key, the eligibility rules still apply to the Facebook account you're connecting. The software isn't the problem. The account is.

Can I schedule a Facebook Live in advance?

Yes, from a Page, through Live Producer on desktop. You can set a date and time, write the description, and Facebook will build an event page so people can get a reminder before you start.

That's a better way to run a live for a local business anyway. Going live cold to an audience that isn't expecting it usually means you're talking to nobody for the first six minutes. Schedule it, post about it twice beforehand, and you'll actually have people on when you start.

Worth knowing: if scheduling isn't showing up for you either, that's another sign you're hitting the access or eligibility wall rather than a bug.

How long does Facebook keep my live video?

30 days. Meta changed this in February 2025, and a lot of businesses still haven't caught up. Your live video sits on your Page for 30 days after it publishes and then it goes away.

So if you're doing weekly lives and treating your Page like an archive, stop. Download the ones worth keeping, and clip the good three minutes out of each stream into a Reel. Facebook even gives you a clip tool for that. The stream is the event, the Reel is the thing that keeps working for you afterward, and Reels get pushed to people who don't follow you yet.

What to do while you're waiting

If you're under 100 followers or inside a restriction window, don't just sit there. Record the video on your phone and post it as a regular video or a Reel. You lose the live chat, and you keep everything else, including the part where people actually see your face and hear how you talk.

Common questions

Why does Facebook say I can't go live yet?
The most common reason is that your account or Page doesn't meet Facebook's eligibility rules. Your Facebook account has to be at least 60 days old, and Pages and profiles in professional mode need at least 100 followers before the Live option turns on. If you clear both of those and still get the error, check that you're actually an admin on the Page and that you're not under a temporary restriction from a past policy strike.

How many followers do you need to go live on Facebook?
100 followers. Facebook rolled that requirement out in June 2024 for Pages and for profiles using professional mode. Once you're past 100, the Live option shows up in the composer. Personal profiles that aren't in professional mode aren't held to the follower rule, but they still have the 60 day account age rule.

Why is the Live video button missing from my Facebook Page?
Either you don't have the right access on the Page, or the Page hasn't met the eligibility rules yet. You need Facebook access with content permissions, or task access to create content, before the Live option appears. Being tagged as an editor in an old Page role setup isn't always enough after Facebook migrated Pages to the new experience.

Can I go live on Facebook from a computer?
Yes, from a Page you can start a live video in your browser or through Meta's Live Producer, and you can also stream in from software like OBS or StreamYard using a stream key. Personal profiles have fewer desktop options. If the browser version won't start, check that you've allowed camera and mic access for facebook.com in your browser settings.

How long does Facebook keep my live video after the stream ends?
30 days. Meta changed the storage policy in February 2025, so live videos are removed from your Page or profile 30 days after they're published. Download anything you want to keep, or clip the good parts into Reels before the window closes.
