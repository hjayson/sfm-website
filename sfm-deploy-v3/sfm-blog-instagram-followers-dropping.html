Why Did My Instagram Followers Drop?

J
Jayson Hines September 16, 2026 · 6 min read

Short answer: almost every Instagram follower drop is Instagram deleting fake and inactive accounts, not real customers walking away from your business. If you lost a chunk overnight and your likes and comments stayed about the same, that was a cleanup. If the number slides down a little every day for weeks, that one is on your content.

I get a version of this text from clients about twice a month. Screenshot of the follower count, and "we lost 40 followers last night, what did we do wrong." Usually the answer is nothing.

Did Instagram delete a bunch of fake accounts?

Instagram runs removal sweeps constantly. Bots, spam accounts, duplicates, accounts that got reported, accounts that have been dead for years. When a sweep hits, the numbers move overnight across a lot of pages at once, which is why you will see other local businesses posting about the same thing on the same day.

Here is how to tell. Take your average likes per post and divide by your follower count, before the drop and after. If that percentage went up, you lost accounts that were never looking at your posts anyway. Your page just got more honest. That is a good day, even though the number on the screen looks worse.

One thing worth saying since it comes up: if somebody ever sold you followers, or you ran one of those follow-for-follow rounds a few years back, expect those to keep evaporating. Instagram gets better at spotting them every year and there is no getting them back.

Why does my follower count drop a little every single day?

That is churn, and some of it is just the cost of having an audience. Losing somewhere around 1 to 3 percent of your followers a month is normal. People clean out their feed, they deactivate accounts, they move, they get tired of seeing posts from a business they bought from once in 2023.

What matters is the trend line over a couple months, not any single day. If you are posting more than ever and the line still points down, the usual reason is that what you post now is not what people followed you for. A restaurant that built its audience on food photos and then switched to daily hiring graphics will bleed followers, and every one of those is a real person making a real choice.

So go back and look at your last 20 posts like a customer would. Would you follow that page?

Did I get shadowbanned or restricted?

A shadowban does not take followers away from you. It hides your posts from people who do not already follow you, which shows up as your reach falling off a cliff while your follower count barely moves. Those are two separate problems with two separate fixes.

If your reach dropped at the same time your followers did, read up on what an Instagram shadowban actually is and how to check for one, and then on why your Instagram reach keeps dropping. If your account itself got hit with a violation, you would normally see a notice in your Account Status, so check there before you assume the worst.

Does losing followers hurt my reach?

Not the way people think. Instagram does not decide how far a post goes based on how many followers you have. It decides based on how the first slice of people who see it respond, so what actually moves your reach is saves, shares, comments, and watch time.

Which is why losing dead accounts can help you. A page with 800 real followers who watch your Reels will out-reach a page with 3,000 followers who are half bots. The bots never engaged, so they were dragging your engagement rate down and telling Instagram your posts were not worth showing to anybody new.

What actually makes followers stick?

Post for the customer you want, not for other business owners. Show faces and show the actual work, and show the town you are in. Around here that means people recognizing the truck in the driveway or the building on the corner, and that beats a stock photo with a quote on it every time.

Then answer people. Comments and DMs within the hour if you can manage it. A follower who gets a reply from an actual human at your business almost never unfollows you.

And keep a rhythm you can hold. Three posts a week forever will do more for you than 12 posts in one week followed by a month of silence. If you would rather hand the posting off than fight it every Sunday night, that is exactly what our social media management for local businesses was built for.

Common questions

How many followers is it normal to lose in a month?
Roughly 1 to 3 percent of your total is normal churn from deactivated accounts, bot removals, and people cleaning up their feed. A slow bleed inside that range is not a problem. A steady decline well past it usually points to your content drifting away from what people followed you for.

Can I get deleted followers back?
If Instagram removed them in a sweep, no, and you do not want them back. Those were fake or abandoned accounts that never saw your posts. Real people who unfollowed can come back on their own, but chasing them is a waste of your time.

Does Instagram tell you who unfollowed you?
No. There is no native list of who unfollowed. Your professional dashboard shows the net change in followers over time, which is the useful number anyway. Be careful with third-party unfollower apps since handing over your login is how accounts get restricted or hacked.

Should I buy followers to make the number look better?
No. Bought followers never engage, so they drag your engagement rate down and tell Instagram your posts are not worth showing to new people. They also get purged eventually, so you pay for a number that disappears.

My followers dropped right after I ran ads. Why?
Ads put you in front of people who never asked to see you, and some of them will unfollow or hide you. That is normal. Follower growth is also the wrong thing to judge an ad by. Judge it on leads, calls, and form fills, since those are what you are actually paying for.
