Why Is My Facebook Ad Account Disabled?

J
Jayson Hines August 21, 2026 · 7 min read

Short answer: Meta disabled it for one of three reasons. A policy violation on an ad or a page tied to the account, a payment that failed or looked suspicious, or account activity that tripped their automated system. Almost none of this gets decided by a person up front. It's software making a call, which is why a lot of disables get reversed once you fix the real thing they flagged and appeal it once, properly.

I've had this happen to client accounts and to my own. The panic is real, especially when you've got leads coming in and the whole thing goes dark on a Thursday morning. So here's what actually gets accounts shut off, how to find the real reason, and how to appeal without burning your one good shot.

How do I find out why Facebook disabled my ad account?

Don't guess. Meta tells you, it's just buried.

Go to Account Quality inside Business Settings. That page shows the restriction level on your ad account, which specific ads got flagged, which policy they say you broke, and whether you have an appeal option open. That's the page that matters. The red banner in Ads Manager is the headline, Account Quality is the actual story.

Also go dig through your email. Meta sends a notice when they disable an account and it lands in spam or the promotions tab constantly. Search for facebookmail.com and support.facebook.com. That email usually names the policy, and you need the exact policy name before you write a single word of an appeal.

What actually gets an ad account disabled?

Policy violations are the most common cause by a mile. And the ones that catch small businesses are rarely the obvious stuff.

Personal attributes language is a big one. If your ad copy says "are you struggling with back pain" or "overweight moms in Toledo", Meta reads that as you claiming to know something personal about the viewer. Health, finances, age, race, religion, all of it. Rewrite it so the ad talks about your business instead of about the reader.

Restricted categories are the other trap. Anything touching credit, housing, employment, or certain financial and health services has to be declared through Meta's special ad category process. Roofers running financing offers and lenders and staffing companies get hit with this all the time. If you didn't declare it, you're violating even when the ad itself is clean.

Then there's the boring stuff that still kills accounts. A card that keeps declining. A brand new business account that suddenly tries to spend a lot. Logging in from a bunch of different places or handing access to a contractor who has other flagged accounts on the same setup. A landing page that doesn't match what the ad promised, or that has a broken form on it. Meta checks the destination, not only the ad.

One thing worth knowing: sometimes it isn't your ad account at all. A restriction on your personal Facebook profile or your business page can cascade down and take the ad account with it. If your ads look clean and you still got hit, check the profile of whoever admins the account.

How do I appeal a disabled Facebook ad account?

Fix the problem first, then appeal. In that order. This is where most people blow it.

Pause or delete the ads that got flagged, correct the copy or the landing page or the payment method, and only then hit the request review button in Account Quality. If you appeal while the violating ad is still sitting there live in your account, you're asking a reviewer to look at the exact thing that triggered the disable.

Keep the appeal short and plain. Say what happened, say what you already changed, and ask for a review. No paragraphs about how long you've been advertising, no arguing that the policy is unfair, no all caps. Reviewers are moving fast. Give them a clean reason to say yes.

Appeal once and wait. Firing off five appeals across five different forms does not speed anything up and it can get you flagged as spam.

Why do appeals get denied?

The number one reason is that the appeal never addressed the violation that was actually cited. Somebody gets disabled for a special ad category issue and writes an appeal about how their ads have always followed the rules. Different subject. Denied.

Second reason is nothing changed. If the account looks identical to the way it looked when the system disabled it, there's nothing for a reviewer to approve.

Third is that the disable is tied to something outside the ad account, like the payment method or the admin's personal profile, and the appeal only talks about the ads.

How long does it take to get reinstated?

First-time cases where you fixed the issue usually come back within a few days. I've seen accounts restored in under an hour and I've seen them sit for two weeks. Repeat violations take longer and get scrutinized harder.

If it's been more than a couple weeks with no movement, try the Meta Business Support chat if you have access to it. Live chat availability depends on your spend history and account type, so not everyone gets it, but when it's there it's the fastest path to a human.

What do I do while I'm locked out?

Don't spin up a brand new ad account on a new profile to get around the block. Meta connects accounts through payment methods, devices, IP addresses, and page admins, and evading a restriction is how a temporary disable turns into a permanent ban on everything you own.

Use the downtime on channels you actually control. Post organically, email your list, call the leads already in your pipeline, and clean up your Google Business Profile. Anything that isn't renting attention from a platform that just took your keys.

This is also the argument for not putting your whole business on one ad account. If Meta going dark for ten days shuts off every lead you get, that's a structural problem with the funnel, not a Facebook problem. We build that mix out for clients as part of our social media marketing and paid ads services, and it's usually the first thing we fix.

How do I keep it from happening again?

Warm up new accounts instead of dumping a big budget in on day one. Keep one solid payment method that doesn't decline. Declare special ad categories when they apply, even when it costs you targeting. Read your own ad copy back and ask whether it's describing your business or describing the person reading it. Keep your landing pages fast, honest, and matching the ad.

And go look at Account Quality once a month even when nothing is wrong. Meta puts warnings there before it puts restrictions there. If you want the bigger picture on running Meta campaigns that hold up, I wrote about whether Facebook ads still work for local businesses.

Common questions

Why did Meta disable my Facebook ad account?
Almost always one of three things: a policy violation on an ad, page, or landing page tied to the account, a payment method that failed or looked suspicious, or unusual account activity that tripped Meta's automated review. Account Quality inside Business Settings shows the specific reason.

How long does it take to get a disabled Facebook ad account back?
First-time cases where you actually fixed the flagged issue usually come back within a few days. Some are restored in under an hour. Repeat violations get reviewed harder and can take a couple of weeks or longer.

Why do Facebook ad account appeals get denied?
The most common reason is that the appeal never addressed the violation Meta cited. The second is that nothing in the account changed before the appeal was filed. Fix the flagged ad, copy, landing page, or payment method first, then request the review.

Can I just make a new Facebook ad account instead of appealing?
No. Meta links accounts through payment methods, devices, IP addresses, and page admins, and treats a new account as ban evasion. That turns a temporary restriction into a permanent one across everything connected to you.

Does a disabled ad account mean my Facebook page is banned too?
Not always, and sometimes it works the other way around. A restriction on your personal profile or your business page can cascade down and disable the ad account. If your ads look clean, check the profile of whoever admins the account.
