Why Are My Google Reviews Not Showing Up?

J
Jayson Hines September 8, 2026 · 7 min read

Short answer: the review is almost always sitting in one of four places. It's still in Google's processing queue (a few minutes to about three days), it got caught by Google's spam filter, it broke one of Google's review rules, or it landed on a different listing than the one you're looking at. Less often, your Business Profile itself has a problem, like a verification flag or a suspension, and Google hides your reviews until that's cleared. None of those mean the review is gone for good, but a couple of them mean it won't come back, and it helps to figure out which one you're dealing with before you spend an hour on hold with support.

This one comes up constantly with the businesses we work with around Toledo and Perrysburg. A customer says "I left you a review!" and you go look and there's nothing there. Or your count says 84 on Monday and 79 on Friday and nobody can tell you why. So this is how I walk clients through it.

Is the review missing, or is it just delayed?

Check this first, because half the time the answer is "wait."

Most Google reviews post within a few minutes. But Google runs every review through an automated check before it goes public, and again after, and that check can hold a review for 24 to 72 hours depending on the reviewer's account history, how many reviews you've gotten lately, and what the review says. If the customer left it this morning, give it until the end of the week before you do anything.

Here's a quick way to see what's really going on. Open your Business Profile dashboard and note your total review count. Then open an incognito or private browser window, search your business name and city, and count what shows publicly. If the dashboard number is higher than the public number, the reviews exist and Google is just not displaying them, which is a different problem than reviews being removed. If both numbers match and they're lower than they used to be, reviews got taken down.

Also ask the customer to look at their own Google profile (Maps, then their profile, then Reviews). If they can see it there and you can't see it on your listing, it's filtered or delayed. If it's gone from their profile too, Google removed it.

One more thing that trips people up: the Google Maps app on a phone caches old data. If a review shows on desktop but not in the app, force close the app or clear its cache. That's not a missing review, that's your phone.

Why did Google remove my review?

Google doesn't send you a note when it pulls a review. You just notice the count changed. The reasons fall into a few buckets.

The spam filter caught it. This is the big one and it catches real reviews all the time. Google's system looks for patterns: several reviews with similar wording, a burst of reviews in a short window, reviews from accounts with no history, reviews left from the same Wi-Fi network or the same physical location as the business, reviews that mention the exact same employee by name. A legitimate review can match one of those patterns by accident. If you had five customers leave reviews from your shop's guest Wi-Fi on the same afternoon, Google may have flagged the whole batch.

The reviewer's account looked fake. A brand new Google account created just to leave you a review is the most common version. So is an account that's been dormant for years, or one that has left a pile of reviews in a short time. Google trusts reviews from people who actually use Google, and it quietly drops the rest.

The review broke a policy. Google has a list, and it's gotten stricter lately. Reviews from employees, family members, vendors, or anybody else with a business tie to you. Reviews that were paid for or rewarded in any way, including a discount or a raffle entry. Reviews that aren't about a real customer experience. Reviews with links, phone numbers, or promotional stuff in them. Google also added rules this year against staff review quotas and against asking customers to name a specific employee, so if your team has been coached to "make sure they mention Mike," that's now a flag.

You were review gating. That's when you ask customers how it went first and only send the Google link to the happy ones. Lots of business owners do this without knowing it's against the rules. Google considers it manipulation and will pull reviews it thinks came through a gated funnel. I get into the right way to ask in how to get more Google reviews for your business, but the short version is you ask everybody the same way and let the chips fall.

And sometimes a competitor or a disgruntled ex-employee reported it. Anybody can flag a review. Google reviews the flag, and it doesn't always get it right.

Why did my review count drop overnight?

When a bunch disappear at once, it's usually one of three things.

A moderation sweep. Google periodically re-runs its filters across old reviews as well as new ones. When it does, reviews that passed a year ago can get pulled now under stricter rules. Businesses that ran a review contest in 2023 or had a vendor "help" with reviews tend to lose a chunk during these sweeps, and those reviews are not coming back.

A bug on Google's end. This has happened for real. Google has had at least one stretch since late 2025 where a problem on their side hid real reviews from public listings for a lot of businesses at the same time. The reviews still existed, they just weren't showing. If your count drops and the Google Business Profile community forum or the r/googlebusinessprofile subreddit is full of people saying the same thing that week, it's not you. Wait it out.

You moved, merged, or transferred. If you recently changed your address, merged a duplicate listing, or moved the profile to a new owner, reviews can take up to a week or so to catch up, and sometimes some don't carry over without a support ticket. Write down your review count and star rating before you make any of those changes so you can prove what's missing.

If your reviews vanished at the same time you lost the ability to respond to reviews or post photos, look at the top of your dashboard. Google may have flagged your profile for re-verification or suspended it. Reviews stay hidden until that's resolved. I covered that whole mess in why is my Google Business Profile suspended.

Why can't my customer see the review they just left?

Beyond the delay and the spam filter, the sneaky one is that they reviewed the wrong listing.

Search your business name on Google Maps and look closely. If there's a second listing with your old address, a slightly different name, or a location Google auto-created that you never claimed, customers can land on that one. Reviews left there won't show on your real profile. If you own the duplicate, claim it and ask Google to merge them. If you don't, use "Suggest an edit" on the duplicate and mark it as a duplicate of your listing. When you contact support about the merge, ask specifically for the reviews to be moved over. They won't do it unless you ask.

The other common one: the customer wrote the review but never hit post. It sounds dumb, but the Google review flow on mobile has a couple of screens, and plenty of people back out at the last one. Ask them to check their own profile. If it's not there either, it never went in.

How do I get a missing Google review back?

You've got two channels, and honesty up front: neither one is fast, and neither one is guaranteed.

1: Gather proof. Screenshots of the review from your email notification or from the customer's own profile, the reviewer's name, the date it was left, the star rating, and what it said. The more specific you are, the better your odds.

2: Have the customer report it from their side. If they can still see the review on their profile but it's not showing on your listing, they can flag it through "Report a problem" on Maps. A report from the reviewer carries more weight than a report from you.

3: Contact Google Business Profile support. Go through the Business Profile help center while signed into the account that manages the listing. Explain what's missing, attach your screenshots, and include both the dashboard count and the public count if they don't match. Keep it short and factual. Replies can take days, and the first reply is often a form letter, so respond and ask for it to be escalated.

4: Know when to stop. If the review was pulled for a real policy reason, an employee reviewed you, it came through a gated link, a customer got a gift card for it, or your cousin wrote it, that review is not coming back, and pushing on it draws attention to the rest of your profile. Let it go and fix the process.

While you're in there, don't confuse this with the opposite problem. If there's a review on your listing that shouldn't be there, that's a different process, and I walked through it in how to remove a fake Google review.

How do I keep my Google reviews from disappearing?

Ask steady, not in bursts. Two or three reviews a week that trickle in for a year look like a healthy business. Forty in one weekend after a text blast looks like a purchase, even when every one of them is real. Send the ask right after each job, one customer at a time.

Ask customers to use their real, everyday Google account. Not a new one, not the one they made for a kid's school login.

Never incentivize. No discounts and no free dessert. You can ask everybody, you just can't pay anybody.

Don't have people review from your building. If you've got a review QR code on the counter, that's fine, but customers scanning it on your Wi-Fi while standing at your register is exactly the pattern the filter looks for. Have them do it in the truck or at home.

Keep your profile clean. Same name, address, and phone number on your website and everywhere else. Complete any verification prompt the day it shows up. Respond to every review, good or bad, because a profile the owner is actively managing gets treated better across the board. Common questions

How long does it take for a Google review to show up?
Usually a few minutes. Google runs every review through an automated check, and that can hold one for 24 to 72 hours if the reviewer's account is new, your profile just got a burst of reviews, or the wording tripped a filter. If it's been more than three days and the customer can still see it on their own profile, it's filtered, not delayed.

Can Google remove a review without telling me?
Yes, and it's the normal way it happens. There's no notification to the business or the reviewer. The only way most owners find out is by noticing the count dropped. Keep a monthly note of your review count and rating so you can spot it.

Why does a review show on my phone but not on my computer, or the other way around?
Caching. The Google Maps app and the browser pull from different caches, and one can lag behind the other by hours. Force close the Maps app or check in an incognito browser window. If the review shows in one place, it exists and it'll sync.

Can I see which reviews Google filtered out?
No. Google doesn't show businesses a list of removed or held reviews. If your dashboard count is higher than your public count, that gap is the number of reviews being held, but you can't see which ones. The only way to identify a specific missing review is from your own records or the customer.

Will my star rating drop if Google removes reviews?
It can, in either direction. Your rating is calculated only from the reviews currently live, so losing a few five-stars can knock a 4.9 down to a 4.7, and losing a one-star can bump it up. That's another reason to keep a steady flow coming in, so a few removals don't swing the number much.
