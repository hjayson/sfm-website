Why Was My Facebook Ad Rejected?

J
Jayson Hines August 31, 2026 · 7 min read

Short answer: Meta's review matched something in your ad against an advertising policy, and most of the time the trigger is the wording, the image, the page you're sending people to, or a problem sitting on your Facebook page rather than the ad itself. Almost all of it is fixable the same day. You either edit the ad so it goes back through review, or you ask for a person to look at it again.

The frustrating part is the notice. It gives you a category, not a sentence. So you sit there staring at an ad that looks completely normal to you, wondering what you did.

How do I find out why my ad was rejected?

Ads Manager will tell you, it's just buried. Open the ad, hover over the Rejected status in the Delivery column, and you'll get the policy that flagged it with a link to the policy page. Meta usually emails you the same thing.

Read the actual policy page, not the one line label. The label is broad on purpose, something like Unacceptable Business Practices or Personal Attributes, and the policy page underneath it is where you find the specific thing that trips the system. That page is also what you're arguing against if you appeal, so you want to know what it says.

Then check Account Quality. That's the section of Business Manager that shows account level and page level problems. If there's a flag sitting in there, the rejected ad is a symptom and editing the headline won't do a thing.

What gets local business ads rejected the most?

By far the number one for local service businesses is personal attributes. That policy covers any copy that implies you know something about the person reading it. "Are you struggling with back pain?" "Tired of being overweight?" "Going through a divorce?" You didn't mean anything by it, but the rule is that your ad can't assert or imply a personal characteristic. The fix is usually small. Talk about what you do instead of what the reader is. "We treat back pain" clears where "Do you have back pain?" doesn't.

Before and after photos are the other big one. Med spas, dentists, weight loss, anything body related. Meta restricts those hard and a side by side image will get pulled even when the results are real and the patient signed off on it.

Then there's the landing page. People forget the review looks at where the click goes. A page that loads slow, throws a full screen popup before anything renders, or promises something different from the ad copy will get the ad rejected even though the ad reads fine.

Special ad categories catch a lot of people too. If you're advertising housing, a job opening, or anything credit related, you have to declare it. A property manager running an apartment ad or a shop running a now hiring ad without setting that category is going to get bounced, and the notice won't say "you forgot the dropdown."

And then the boring stuff that still gets you: using another company's logo or brand name in the creative, claiming a partnership you don't have, or a page that already has an unresolved policy issue on it. When it's the page, every ad you build from it fails no matter how clean the copy is. That's a different animal, and I went through it in more detail in why your Facebook ad account gets disabled.

Should I edit the ad or request a review?

Depends on whether you actually broke the rule.

If you read the policy and can see what tripped it, just edit. Change the line, swap the image, fix the destination, save it. The ad goes right back into the queue and usually gets looked at fast. You don't need permission for that and you don't burn anything.

If you read the policy and you're confident the ad is clean, request review. In Ads Manager you select the rejected ad and hit request review, then explain in plain language why it should run. Meta says most of those come back in about a day. Sometimes they take a week.

One thing to not do: duplicate a rejected ad and run it again with nothing changed. Same ad, same result, and you've used up the appeal on it. Once an ad gets rejected on review a second time, that ad is finished.

Why does my ad keep getting rejected when nothing is wrong with it?

The first pass is automated and it's matching patterns, not reading your intent. A chiropractor writing about back pain isn't doing anything shady, but the system has seen a hundred thousand versions of that ad that were, so it flags the shape of it.

The other reason is that the problem isn't in the ad at all. It's the page, the account, the domain, or the URL you're sending traffic to. If every single ad from one account keeps failing while the same copy runs fine somewhere else, stop rewriting headlines. Go look at Account Quality.

Does a rejected ad hurt my ad account?

One rejection, no. Meta expects some of that, which is why the appeal exists in the first place.

A stack of them over a short window is a different story. Account standing tracks repeat violations, and the pattern that gets accounts restricted is somebody submitting slight variations of the same ad over and over until one slips through. That looks exactly like what somebody trying to get around the rules would do, because it is. Fix the real cause once and move on.

What I actually do when a client's ad gets pulled

Same routine every time. Read the policy page it linked, not the summary. Open the landing page on my phone and watch what happens in the first two seconds, since half of these turn out to be a popup or a redirect nobody looked at. Reread the primary text out loud listening for any sentence that points at the reader instead of at the business. Check Account Quality for anything sitting at the page or account level.

Nine times out of ten it's one word in the headline or a page that misbehaves on mobile. Fix, resubmit, running by lunch.

Common questions

How long does Facebook take to review an ad?
Most ads clear review inside 24 hours and plenty go through in under an hour. Edits to an ad that was already flagged tend to sit longer, and weekends are slower. If something has been stuck in review for more than a day, check Account Quality before you assume it is just a queue.

Can I appeal a rejected Facebook ad twice?
No. Once you request review on an ad and it comes back rejected again, that ad is done. Build a new one with the actual problem fixed instead of resubmitting the same thing.

Will editing my rejected ad restart the learning phase?
A meaningful edit does reset learning. That barely matters on an ad that was rejected before it ever delivered, since there was no performance data to lose in the first place.

Why did my ad get approved and then rejected later?
Meta keeps reviewing ads while they run, and it checks your landing page too. Either something changed on the destination page or a later pass caught what the first one missed.

Does having ads rejected get my Facebook page banned?
One or two rejections will not. Repeat violations of the same policy are what push it up to page level or account level restrictions, which is why fixing the real reason beats resubmitting variations.
