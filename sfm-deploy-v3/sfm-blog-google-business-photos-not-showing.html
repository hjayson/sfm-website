Why Are My Google Business Profile Photos Not Showing Up?

J
Jayson Hines September 2, 2026 · 7 min read

Short answer: your photos are almost always sitting in Google's review queue, or they got rejected. Google looks at every image before it goes live on your profile, and most uploads show up within 24 to 48 hours. If it's been longer than about three days, the photo didn't pass, and the reason is usually image quality, a logo or text laid over the shot, or Google not being able to tell that the picture has anything to do with your business.

The frustrating part is that Google doesn't tell you. There's no rejection email and no little red flag in the dashboard. The photo just quietly never appears. So here's how to figure out which one it is and what to do about it.

How long does it take for Google Business Profile photos to show up?

Google says up to 24 hours in most cases, and in practice I've seen photos go live in ten minutes and I've seen them take two full days. A weekend upload tends to sit longer.

The one thing that trips people up is checking while they're logged in. If you're signed in to the account that owns the profile, you'll often see your own upload in the dashboard even though nobody else can. Open an incognito window, or grab your phone and search your business name while signed out. That's the real test. If the photo's not there for a stranger, it's not there.

Past three days, stop waiting. It's not slow, it's gone.

Why did Google reject my photo?

Here's what actually gets pulled, in rough order of how often I run into it with local businesses.

Graphics instead of photos. This is the big one. You made a nice square in Canva with your logo, your phone number, and "20% off fall tune-ups" across the front, and you uploaded it as a photo. Google doesn't want that. The photo section is meant for pictures of the actual place, so anything that reads as an ad or a flyer gets stripped out. Put the promo in a Google post instead, that's what posts are for.

Text or a watermark over a real photo. Same problem, smaller version. Your photographer's watermark in the corner or your logo stamped on the bottom can be enough to get an otherwise good shot rejected.

Quality. Blurry, dark, tilted, taken through a windshield. Google wants images that are in focus and well lit, at least 720 by 720 pixels, saved as a JPG or PNG. A modern phone camera clears that easily as long as you hold still and there's decent light.

Heavy filters. If it looks obviously edited, Google reads it as not representative of the business. Light color correction is fine. A moody preset that turns your lobby purple is not.

Stock photos. Google can tell. That generic smiling handshake image lives on ten thousand other websites, and it has no connection to your address. Same goes for pictures pulled off a manufacturer's site.

Photos that don't match the business. A shot of a truck on a highway for a dental office, a screenshot of your website, a group photo from a conference in another state. If Google can't connect the image to what you do or where you are, it's a coin flip.

Is your profile actually verified?

Photos and videos don't show publicly until the Business Profile itself is verified. If you're brand new, or you moved locations, or you changed your business name and Google kicked you back into re-verification, everything you upload will sit in limbo until that clears.

Check the verification status in your dashboard before you spend an afternoon troubleshooting image files. And if your profile got suspended rather than just unverified, that's a different animal with a different fix, which I walked through in what to do when your Google Business Profile is suspended.

Why do my photos show on desktop but not on my phone?

Usually caching. Google's mobile app and the search results page don't always refresh at the same speed, and your phone may be holding an older version of the profile. Force close the app, or check in a private browser tab.

There's also a display thing worth knowing. Google decides which photos to show first and it heavily favors customer photos over owner photos for a lot of business types. So your photos might be live and just buried behind fifteen pictures customers took. Tap through to the full photo gallery and look under the "By owner" tab before you assume anything is missing.

Where did my old photos go?

Photos that were live for months can disappear later. A few reasons that happens:

Google ran a sweep and retroactively removed images that break the current guidelines, which happens more than people realize. Somebody with access to the profile deleted them, and on a business with multiple managers that's worth checking. Or the profile itself got merged, duplicated, or suspended, and the photos went with it.

If a large batch vanished at once, don't re-upload the same files yet. Figure out whether the profile is healthy first, otherwise you'll just watch the new uploads disappear too.

What should I upload instead?

The stuff that sticks is boring and real. Your storefront from the road, so people recognize it when they pull up. The inside. Your team. Your trucks or your equipment. Before and after shots of actual jobs you did. A few pictures of the parking situation if that's ever confused anybody.

Shoot them on a phone, in daylight, without editing them into oblivion. Upload a handful at a time rather than forty at once, since a giant batch of uploads from a new profile sometimes gets more scrutiny. And keep adding a few every month. Google notices profiles that stay active, and photos are one of the easiest signals to keep feeding.

When it's not really about the photos

Sometimes the photo problem is the symptom. If your images keep getting pulled, your posts aren't showing, and you're not turning up in the map results for searches you should own, the profile itself needs attention. Common questions

How long does it take for Google Business Profile photos to show up?
Usually within 24 to 48 hours, and sometimes right away. Google reviews every upload before it goes public. If a photo still isn't visible after about three days, treat it as rejected or held rather than slow, and go check the photo itself.

Why does Google keep rejecting my photos?
The common reasons are low quality, images that look edited or filtered, photos with logos, text, or promo graphics laid over them, stock photography, and anything Google can't tie to your actual business location. Upload real, clear, unedited photos of your place, your team, and your work.

What are the photo requirements for a Google Business Profile?
Google wants JPG or PNG files, at least 720 by 720 pixels, in focus and well lit, with no heavy filters or added graphics. Bigger files are fine. Screenshots, flyers, and anything with text baked into the image are the ones that get pulled.

Why can I see my photos when I'm logged in but customers can't?
Because you're seeing your own pending upload. Owners can often see a photo in the dashboard while it's still in review or after it's been rejected. Always check in an incognito window or on a phone that isn't signed in to your business account.

Can I get a rejected Google Business Profile photo back?
Not directly. There's no appeal button for a single photo. Your options are to re-upload a cleaner version of the same shot, or contact Google Business Profile support through the Help menu in your dashboard if a batch of good photos keeps getting pulled.
