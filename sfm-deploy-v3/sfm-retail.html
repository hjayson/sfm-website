Common Questions

FAQs for Retail & Local Business

Do I need an e-commerce store for this to work?

No, most of our retail clients are brick-and-mortar focused. We drive foot traffic with local ads and content. E-commerce is a bonus if you have it, not a requirement.

What kind of content works best for retail?

Behind-the-scenes, product reveals, owner stories, customer features, and community involvement. Real and relatable consistently outperforms polished and corporate for local retailers.

How do you measure success for a retail store?

We track foot traffic attribution, coupon redemptions, online inquiries, follower growth, and ad-attributed sales. We set clear goals on your strategy call and report against them monthly.

Can you help me compete during the holiday season?

Absolutely, that's where we shine. We build out your full Q4 strategy months in advance so you're not scrambling during the busiest retail season of the year.

HOW WE WORK WITH SHOPS

Marketing that matches
what you actually bought.

Content built from your buy, not from a trend list

You committed to this season months ago at market. The job is moving what arrived, so the content follows your actual assortment and what has strong sell-through, rather than whatever is trending on an app that week. Promoting something you only have two of is worse than not promoting at all.

Q4 built starting in early fall

If the holiday stretch is close to half your year, the audience for it cannot be assembled in December. We start building in September and October so that Small Business Saturday and the weeks after it land on people who already know you, already follow you, and have already been in the store once.

Clearing dead stock without training people to wait for markdowns

Cash tied up in inventory that will not move is the quiet killer, and once markdowns eat into a keystone markup there is not much margin left. Clearance campaigns need to be built so they move the stuck product without teaching regulars that everything eventually goes on sale.

Listing hygiene that keeps earned customers from bouncing

Hours that match reality, current photos, and some visibility into what is actually in stock. Somebody deciding whether to drive over is looking for a reason to trust that the trip is worth it, and a stale listing or a wrong closing time undoes all the work that got them interested.

WHY RETAIL IS DIFFERENT

You bought your Christmas inventory in June.
Your marketing should know that.

A store is not a service business. You have already spent the money, months ago, on a guess about what people would want. Everything after that is trying to move what is on the shelf.

Your cash is sitting on the shelf

You wrote the holiday buy in early summer, paid vendor minimums, and now that money is inventory whether it sells or not. Dead stock is not just a write-off at the end of the season, it is cash you already spent and cannot use. Marketing has to move what you actually bought, not whatever happens to photograph well.

Q4 is not a quarter, it is the year

For a lot of independent shops the holiday stretch is close to half of annual sales, and Small Business Saturday alone can be a meaningful chunk of it. That concentration means a soft November is not something you make up in March. The audience you need in December has to be built in September and October.

Posting is not the same as giving someone a reason to drive over

Every shop owner has run the sale post three times, collected forty likes, and watched two people come in. Likes are not foot traffic. A post that moves people has a specific reason, a deadline, and a reason to choose you over the same thing on Amazon arriving tomorrow.

Wrong hours on Google cost you customers you already earned

Someone drove out from Sylvania because your listing said six and you closed at five. That customer is not coming back, and they may tell people. Accurate hours, current stock, and recent photos are unglamorous and they quietly decide whether a browser becomes a visit.
