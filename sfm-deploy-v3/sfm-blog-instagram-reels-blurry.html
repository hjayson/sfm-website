Why Are My Instagram Reels Blurry?

J
Jayson Hines September 21, 2026 · 7 min read

Short answer: it's almost always compression. Instagram re-encodes every video you upload so it can stream fast on a bad connection, and if the file you handed it was already small, already squeezed once, or shot at a lower resolution, there's nothing good left for it to work with. The fix is mostly about what you upload, not a setting you flip after the fact.

I get asked this constantly. Somebody shoots a clean video, it looks sharp in their camera roll, they post it, and it comes out looking like it was filmed through a screen door. Here's what's actually going on and what to do about it.

What does Instagram do to your video after you hit share?

It compresses it. Every single time. Instagram has to serve your Reel to somebody standing in a parking lot with one bar, so it shrinks the file and re-encodes it into a few different versions.

Compression itself is not the problem. Compression on top of compression is. If you shot a video, edited it in one app, exported it, ran it through a second app for captions, exported again, then texted it to yourself before posting, Instagram is the fourth or fifth thing to squeeze that file. By then the detail is gone and no setting brings it back.

Why does it look fine on my phone but blurry on Instagram?

Because your camera roll is playing the original file. Full size, full quality, straight off your phone's storage. Instagram is playing a re-encoded copy that got streamed to you over the internet.

So the comparison you're making isn't fair to Instagram. That said, the gap shouldn't be huge. If your original looks crisp and the posted version looks like mush, something in your workflow is knocking quality down before Instagram ever touches it.

Is it blurry for everyone, or just on my phone?

Worth checking before you delete the post. A few things make a Reel look worse on your end specifically:

Data Saver is on. If you're on cellular with Data Saver enabled, Instagram deliberately serves you a lower quality version. Turn it off in Settings and Activity, then Data usage and media quality.

You're on weak signal. Instagram drops resolution on the fly when your connection is slow, same as Netflix does. Get on wifi and look again.

It just finished processing. Right after you post, Instagram sometimes serves a lower res version while the high quality one is still being made. Give it fifteen or twenty minutes before you judge it. A lot of people delete a perfectly good Reel three minutes after posting.

What size and settings should I be using?

Shoot and export at 1080 by 1920, vertical, 9:16. MP4 file, H.264 video, AAC audio, 30 frames per second. That's the boring target and it covers almost everybody.

Going bigger than 1080 wide does not help. Instagram caps out there for Reels, so a 4K export just gets crushed harder on the way in. If you shot in 4K, export a 1080p version yourself so you control the downscale instead of letting Instagram do it.

Also turn on the high quality upload setting. Same menu as before: Settings and Activity, Data usage and media quality, then switch on the option for uploading at the highest quality. It doesn't fix a bad file, but leaving it off guarantees a worse one.

What's quietly killing your quality before you even upload

This is where most of the real damage happens, and none of it is Instagram's fault.

Sending the video to yourself through a messaging app first. Texting or emailing a video compresses it hard. If you filmed on one phone and edited on a laptop, move the file with AirDrop, a cable, or a cloud folder. Never through a message thread.

Screen recording. If you screen record a video to grab it, you're re-capturing an already compressed playback at whatever resolution your screen is. It's a copy of a copy.

Downloading your own video back off another platform. Pulling your TikTok down and reposting it to Reels means you're uploading TikTok's compressed version, and you usually drag a watermark along with it. Always keep and post the original export.

Bad light. This one surprises people. A dim room forces your camera to crank the ISO, which creates grain, and grain is the single hardest thing for a compressor to handle. It burns all the data on random noise instead of your face. Filming next to a window will do more for your video quality than any export setting.

Lots of fast motion. Quick pans, confetti, busy backgrounds, heavy transitions. All of it eats bitrate. Slower shots hold up way better after compression.

Why do the first few seconds look worse than the rest?

Instagram starts playback before the whole file is ready, so it leads with a lower quality chunk and steps up once it has buffered. That's normal and there's nothing to fix.

It does mean your first frame is often the roughest looking one, which is a problem when that frame is your hook. If you're putting text on screen in the first second, make it big and high contrast so it reads even when the video hasn't sharpened up yet.

Why did my old Reel get blurrier over time?

You're not imagining it. Instagram keeps the high quality version of a video active while people are watching it, and older videos that stop getting views can get served from a lower quality version instead. Head of Instagram Adam Mosseri has talked about this publicly.

Nothing to do about it on a post that's already cooled off. It's also a decent argument for not obsessing over one Reel and just posting the next one.

Why your text and logo look fuzzy specifically

Sharp edges are what compression hates most. Thin fonts, small text, a logo with fine lines, all of it turns to mush faster than a face does.

Use heavier font weights, bump the size up, and keep strong contrast between the text and whatever is behind it. White text on a busy background is the worst combination there is. If you're building templates for a brand, design them assuming they'll get compressed, because they will.

When blurry isn't actually your problem

Here's the part nobody wants to hear. I've watched business owners spend three weeks chasing video quality on a Reel that got 200 views, when the reason it got 200 views had nothing to do with sharpness.

Quality matters up to a point. Past that point it's the hook, the topic, and whether the thing is worth watching for eight seconds. If your Reels are clean and still going nowhere, the problem is further upstream. That's usually where our social media management work starts, figuring out what to say before worrying about how it looks. If you want the reach side of this, I wrote about why Instagram Reels get no views separately.

Fix the file, then go make a better video.

Common questions

What's the best resolution for Instagram Reels?
1080 by 1920 pixels, vertical 9:16, exported as an MP4 with H.264 video at 30 frames per second. Uploading something larger than 1080 wide doesn't help since Instagram caps Reels there and will just compress it harder on the way in.

Does turning on high quality uploads actually fix blurry Reels?
It helps, but it won't rescue a file that was already compressed. Turn it on under Settings and Activity, then Data usage and media quality. Then fix the bigger issue, which is usually how many times the video got exported or sent around before you posted it.

Why does my Reel look sharp in my camera roll but blurry on Instagram?
Your camera roll plays the original file. Instagram plays a re-encoded copy that had to be streamed to you. Some quality loss is normal. A huge gap means the file lost quality somewhere in your editing or transfer process before Instagram got it.

Should I wait before deleting a blurry Reel?
Yes. Instagram often serves a lower quality version for the first several minutes while it finishes processing the good one. Give it fifteen or twenty minutes, check it on wifi with Data Saver off, and then decide.

Can I repost my TikTok video to Reels without losing quality?
Not if you download it from TikTok first. That file is already compressed and usually watermarked. Keep your original export from whatever you edited in and upload that same file to each platform separately.
