Why Is My Google Business Profile Suspended?

J
Jayson Hines August 19, 2026 · 9 min read

Short answer: Google suspended your profile because something on it tripped a spam filter, and the fix is to correct that thing first and then file one appeal with proof. Do not create a new profile. Do not appeal five times. Fix, then appeal once.

The part nobody tells you is that a suspension usually isn't a punishment for cheating. Most of the local businesses I've helped through this never broke a rule on purpose. They edited their address and phone number on the same afternoon, or a contractor listed a home address, or somebody stuffed a couple keywords into the business name three years ago and it finally caught up. Google's detection got a lot more aggressive over the past year, and newer profiles are getting caught the hardest.

What does it actually mean when Google says my profile is suspended?

There are two flavors and they feel very different.

In the milder one, your listing still shows up on Search and Maps, and customers can still find you and leave reviews. What you lost is control. The profile reads as unverified and you can't edit it. Bad, but the lights are still on.

The other kind pulls your listing off Search and Maps completely. Customers type your name and you're not there. That's the one where the phone stops ringing that same week, and it's the version most people mean when they say they got suspended.

Quick heads up if you end up talking to Google support: the terms "soft suspension" and "hard suspension" get thrown around constantly in SEO circles, but they're not official Google language. The support rep may have no idea what you're talking about. Just describe what you're seeing instead.

Why did Google suspend my profile if I didn't do anything wrong?

Because the system is automated and it doesn't know your intentions. It sees a pattern that matches how fake listings behave and it acts.

The single most common trigger I run into is editing too much at once. Somebody sits down on a Sunday and updates the business name, the address, the phone number, and the primary category in one session. To you that's cleanup. To Google that looks exactly like somebody hijacking a listing.

Here's what actually pulls the trigger most often:

Keyword stuffing in the business name. Your sign says "Miller Plumbing." Your listing says "Miller Plumbing Toledo Emergency Drain Repair." That's a violation, full stop, and it's one of the easiest for Google to spot. The name field has to match your real-world signage.

An address you can't defend. A P.O. box, a UPS Store mailbox, a virtual office, a coworking desk you rent by the hour. If a customer can't walk in during your posted hours and find you there, it's a problem. Same goes for a home address on a business that should be set up as a service area business instead.

Duplicate listings. Two profiles for the same business, usually because somebody at the shop created one years ago and forgot, or a previous marketing company made one. Google finds the pair and suspends.

Your information doesn't match across the web. If your name, address, and phone read one way on your website, another way on Yelp, and a third way on an old directory, that inconsistency reads as a signal that the business isn't real.

Review manipulation. Buying reviews, trading them with another business, or pushing a big batch of them through in a couple days. I wrote about the right way to do this in how to actually rank on Google Maps, and the short version is that steady and real beats fast every time.

Sometimes it's also just the category. Locksmiths, garage door repair, towing, plumbing, and a handful of other trades get scrutinized far harder than everybody else because those categories have been abused for years. If you're in one of them, you're playing on a tighter rope through no fault of your own.

Should I just make a new profile instead?

No. This is the one thing I'd beg you not to do.

Creating a replacement profile is itself a violation, and Google is good at connecting the new listing to the suspended one. You'll usually end up with two suspended profiles instead of one, and now your appeal is harder because you've added a second offense on top of whatever the original problem was.

You also throw away every review you ever earned. Those don't transfer. Years of five-star ratings, gone, because starting over felt faster than appealing.

Appeal the profile you have.

How do I get my Google Business Profile reinstated?

Order matters more than anything else here. An appeal is not a resubmission, it's a case you're making, and you only really get one good swing at it.

Fix the violation before you file. Go through the profile like an auditor. Strip the keywords out of your business name so it matches your sign exactly. If your address won't hold up, either change it to a location that will or convert the listing to a service area business and hide the address. Find and remove duplicate listings. Then go clean up your name, address, and phone everywhere else on the web so they all read identically down to the "Suite B."

Then gather proof that you're a real business at that location. A business license. A utility bill with the address on it. Photos of your permanent exterior signage, your entrance, and the inside of the space. Registration paperwork. The more of this you have, the better your odds.

Then file the appeal through Google's appeals tool, once, and explain plainly what the issue was and what you corrected. Don't argue and don't get emotional about it. A calm factual appeal with documents attached beats an angry one every time.

Then wait. This is the hard part and I'm not going to sugarcoat it. Filing again because you're impatient is how good cases turn into denied ones, and stacked denials make the whole thing tougher to unwind.

If you get asked for video verification, know that the requirements got strict. They want one continuous shot with no cuts, starting outside at your street sign, walking to your entrance so the permanent signage is visible, and then you opening the door and stepping inside. Any edit or stop in that video and you're doing it again.

How long does reinstatement take?

Most straightforward cases land somewhere in the three to fourteen business day range. Messier ones, especially anything involving duplicates or a disputed address, can stretch to a month or more. There's no promised timeline, and it moves with however backed up the review queue is that week.

Plan for it to be weeks, not days, and make other arrangements for leads in the meantime. If your listing is fully off Maps, that's real revenue you're losing while you wait, and it's worth turning something else on to bridge the gap.

What happens to my reviews while I'm suspended?

They're usually fine. If the profile gets reinstated, reviews and photos typically come back with it, which is the whole reason starting a new listing is such a bad trade.

Where people lose reviews is when they delete the suspended profile out of frustration, or when they build a new one and let the old one die. Leave it alone and appeal it.

How do I keep this from happening again?

Make one change at a time and leave a week between edits. If you need to update your address and your phone number, do the address, wait, confirm nothing broke, then do the phone. It feels absurdly slow. It's also the single easiest way to avoid ever going through this.

Keep your name, address, and phone identical everywhere online, and audit that once a year. Keep your business name matching your actual sign, even when a competitor is getting away with stuffing theirs. Their listing being up today doesn't mean it'll be up in March.

And take five minutes to check that you still have owner access to the profile. A surprising number of businesses around here find out during a crisis that the only owner on the account is a marketing company they fired in 2023, or a former employee whose email got shut off. Fix that while things are calm.

Common questions

How long does a Google Business Profile reinstatement take?
Most straightforward cases are resolved in roughly three to fourteen business days. Complicated ones, especially anything involving duplicate listings or a disputed address, can run a month or longer. Google does not promise a timeline, and filing repeat appeals while you wait tends to hurt your case rather than speed it up.

Will I lose my reviews if my profile is suspended?
Usually not. When a profile is reinstated, the reviews and photos normally come back with it. The way businesses actually lose reviews is by deleting the suspended profile or abandoning it to build a new listing, since reviews do not transfer between profiles.

Can I just create a new Google Business Profile instead of appealing?
No. Creating a replacement for a suspended profile is itself a policy violation, and Google generally connects the new listing back to the old one. You usually end up with two suspended profiles, a weaker appeal, and none of your original reviews.

Does a suspension hurt my Google Maps ranking after I get reinstated?
Rankings often dip for a stretch after reinstatement while the profile re-establishes itself, then recover. The bigger hit is the time you spent invisible. Keep collecting reviews and keep your information consistent everywhere, and the recovery goes faster.

Do I need a physical address to have a Google Business Profile?
Not necessarily, but you do need to set it up honestly. If customers come to you, you need a real location staffed during your posted hours. If you travel to customers, you should be set up as a service area business with the address hidden. Using a P.O. box, a mailbox service, or a virtual office as a storefront is a common reason profiles get suspended.
