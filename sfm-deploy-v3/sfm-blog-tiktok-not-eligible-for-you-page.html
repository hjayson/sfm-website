Why Does TikTok Say My Video Is Not Eligible for the For You Page?

J
Jayson Hines September 9, 2026 · 7 min read

Short answer: TikTok reviewed your video and decided it will not push it to strangers on the For You feed. The video is still on your profile and your followers can still watch it, but the algorithm won't recommend it, which for most business accounts means it's basically dead. The notice always has a reason attached, so before you delete anything, tap it and read what TikTok actually flagged. For a local business it's nearly always one of four things: a clip that isn't yours (watermarks, reposted content), a promo video you didn't mark as commercial content, something TikTok calls low quality (a still image, a two second clip, a slideshow with nothing in it), or something in the video that bumps into their recommendation rules even though it's allowed to stay up.

This comes up a lot with the businesses we run TikTok for, and the first move is almost always wrong. People see the notice, panic, delete the video, and repost the same file with a different caption. That loses the only explanation TikTok gave you and usually gets flagged again anyway. So here's how to handle it in order.

What does "not eligible for the For You feed" actually mean?

TikTok has two different bars for a video. The first is whether it can stay on the app at all. The second, and it's a higher bar, is whether TikTok is willing to recommend it to people who don't follow you. Your video cleared the first bar and missed the second.

So the video isn't removed. Nobody got a strike. It's just not going into the For You feed, and TikTok also says videos in that state can be harder to find in search. Since the For You feed is where nearly all business reach comes from on TikTok, that's the whole ballgame.

It also isn't a guess on their part. This is an explicit decision with a category attached to it, which is different from a video that simply didn't take off. I wrote about the plain low-view problem separately in why your TikTok videos are getting no views. If you got the notice, you're in this article, not that one.

Where do I find the reason TikTok flagged it?

Three places, and you want to check all of them because the message shows up differently depending on whether it's one video or the whole account.

1: Open the video itself. Tap the three dots, then Analytics or the video status area. The notice with the reason category usually sits right there at the top. 2: Check your Inbox. TikTok sends a system notification when a video or account is marked ineligible, and that notification is also where the Appeal button lives. 3: Run Account check. In TikTok Studio go to More tools, then Account check, or go through Settings and privacy, Support, Safety Center, Account check. A checkmark means nothing is flagged. A warning sign means there's an issue on the account or a specific post.

Screenshot the notice before you touch anything. Copy the video link and note the day you posted it. Save the original file and your edit project too if you have one. Sounds like overkill for a 20 second video. It isn't, because if you end up appealing, that's your evidence, and if you delete the video the notice goes with it.

Why did TikTok flag my video?

The notice tells you the category, and the category maps to a fairly short list. Here's what actually gets local business videos flagged.

The footage isn't yours. This is the number one reason I see. A reposted clip from another account, a video with someone else's watermark or logo still on it, a screen recording of a news segment, a trending clip you downloaded and put your logo over. TikTok's guidelines specifically call out reproduced content without new or creative changes, and content with another creator's visible watermark, as not eligible for recommendation. Cropping out the watermark does not fix it. If someone else's clip is still the substance of the video, it's still their clip.

You're promoting something and didn't disclose it. TikTok has a content disclosure toggle for when you're marketing a product, a brand, or a business. If the video is a straight promo (here's our special, come in this weekend, book now) and that toggle is off, TikTok lists undisclosed commercial content as ineligible for the feed. A hashtag like #ad does not count. It has to be the setting. This one catches business accounts constantly because they figure it's their own business so it doesn't apply. It applies.

TikTok calls it low quality. Their examples are extremely short clips, videos built entirely from GIFs, still images, and slideshows with no real content. A 3 second logo bump with music, a flyer turned into a video, a photo with text over it, a slideshow of stock images. Those all count.

The content hits a For You feed standard even though it's allowed on the app. This is the one people don't expect. TikTok's recommendation rules are stricter than their removal rules and they cover a bunch of categories: health claims, weight loss and before/after style content, anything that looks like a scam or a giveaway with fine print, dangerous activities, regulated products like alcohol or tobacco or firearms, and mature themes. A med spa posting a treatment result, a bar posting a drink special, a gun shop posting inventory, a chiropractor implying a cure. All of those can be fine on your profile and still get kept out of the feed.

Your account is new or has a pattern. Brand new accounts get scrutinized harder on early uploads. And if you've had a few flags for the same reason, TikTok starts treating the account, not just the video, as unsuitable to recommend.

Is this the same as a shadowban?

No, and the difference matters for what you do next. Shadowban is what people call any sudden drop in reach with no explanation. What you have is different. TikTok is telling you exactly what it did and giving you a button to argue with it. If you've got low views and no notice anywhere, you're dealing with a plain content problem, and that's the no views article again. If you've got the notice, stop guessing and read it.

How do I appeal a video that's not eligible for recommendation?

Only appeal if you think TikTok got it wrong. If the video really is a reposted clip or a promo with the disclosure off, an appeal just wastes the review and you'll be waiting on a no.

For a single video, open the notice on the video or in your Inbox and tap Appeal. For an account level restriction, go to Inbox, open the "ineligible for recommendation" notification, tap View more, look through the affected videos, and tap Appeal. You can also get there from your profile, where the notice sits right under your username.

Keep the appeal short and factual. Say what you made, that you own the footage, what the video actually shows, and why the category doesn't fit. Something like: "This is original footage I shot in my shop showing how we install a water heater. There's no third party content and no unsafe activity. I believe it was flagged in error." Don't write a paragraph about how unfair it is. Nobody reads that.

Then leave it alone. Don't delete it and don't upload the same file three more times to "test." An open appeal on a video you deleted is an appeal on nothing.

Should I delete the video and repost it?

Not as a first move, and not with the same file. If the notice is wrong, appeal. If the notice is right, the fix is a genuinely different video, not the same one with a filter, a crop, a speed change, or a new caption. TikTok doesn't publish any rule saying cosmetic edits restore eligibility, and in practice the same file gets the same flag.

Take the reposted clip example. Say you grabbed a 30 second piece of a podcast about your industry, cut it, added your logo. That got flagged as unoriginal. Cropping the watermark off that clip doesn't count as a remake. A real remake is you on camera reacting to what they said, with maybe a few seconds of the original clip if you have the rights to use it, and your own take as the main content. That's a new video. It's also a better video.

For the promo example the remake is easier. Same video, disclosure toggle turned on before you post. That one's a two second fix as long as you actually post it fresh with the setting on.

What if my whole account says ineligible for recommendation?

That means TikTok saw enough flagged videos to stop recommending anything from the account, and search visibility drops too. You'll see it in Inbox and under your username on your profile. Same appeal path as above, but before you appeal, go through your recent uploads honestly. If half of them are reposted trending clips, the appeal is going to lose. Pull the videos that clearly violate the category, fix the pattern going forward, and then appeal on the ones that were legitimately yours.

It also raises the question of whether TikTok is where this business should be spending its energy at all. I get into that in should my business be on TikTok. For a lot of local businesses the answer is yes but not as the main channel, and an account restriction is a decent moment to rethink the mix.

How do I keep it from happening again?

Most of it is a checklist you run before you hit post. Shoot your own footage, or make sure whatever you use is licensed and actually transformed by what you add. Turn the content disclosure on for anything that promotes the business, even a soft promo. Skip the 3 second logo bumps and the flyer slideshows. If you're in a category TikTok is touchy about (health, aesthetics, alcohol, firearms, anything with money or results claims) learn where the line is for the feed and stay on the right side of it, because "it's allowed" and "it'll get recommended" are two different things there.

And if your team is posting for the business and keeps tripping over this, that's usually a sign nobody owns the process. That's a big part of what we handle in our social media management for local businesses: the shooting, the editing, the settings, and the part where somebody checks the account status before it turns into a restriction. Either way, read the notice before you do anything else. It's the one time TikTok tells you exactly what's wrong.

Common questions

How long does a TikTok appeal take?
TikTok doesn't publish a set turnaround. Most of the ones we've seen come back inside a couple of days, some take a week or more. Write down the day you submitted it and leave the video alone until you hear back. Deleting it while the appeal is open kills the appeal.

Will my video get views again if the appeal is approved?
Sometimes, but don't count on it picking up where it left off. An approved appeal puts the video back in the pool for recommendation. It doesn't hand it the momentum it lost while it sat there. If the video is time sensitive, plan on making a fresh one either way.

Does removing #fyp or changing the caption fix it?
No. There's no TikTok rule that says taking off a hashtag or rewriting the caption reverses an eligibility decision. The notice names a reason. Fix that reason. Caption and hashtag edits are fine to do anyway, they just aren't the fix.

Does one flagged video affect my other videos?
One video on its own usually doesn't. Several in a row that get flagged for the same reason can, because TikTok says repeated unsuitable posts can make the whole account ineligible for recommendation and harder to find in search. That's why it pays to fix the pattern instead of just the one clip.

Why did it say not eligible right after I posted and then go back to normal?
New uploads get an automated review and the status can flip while that runs, especially on newer accounts. If the notice disappears on its own inside an hour or so and the video starts pulling normal views, it was just the review. If the notice sticks around, treat it as a real decision and open it to read the reason.
