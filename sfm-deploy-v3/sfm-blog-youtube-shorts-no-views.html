Why Are My YouTube Shorts Not Getting Views?

J
Jayson Hines August 15, 2026 · 7 min read

Short answer: your Shorts aren't getting views because people are swiping away in the first two or three seconds, so YouTube stops showing them to anybody new. Views on Shorts don't get handed out based on your subscriber count or how long you've been posting. Every single Short gets dropped in front of a small test audience, and what happens in those first few seconds decides whether it gets a bigger one. If nobody sticks around, it dies right there.

That's the whole thing in one paragraph. But there are a handful of reasons people fail that test, and a couple of them have nothing to do with your video being bad. Here's what I actually check when a client's Shorts flatline.

Why did my Shorts suddenly stop getting views?

If you were pulling a few thousand views a video and now you're getting 40, the first place to look isn't the algorithm. It's what changed on your end.

Usually something did. Maybe you switched topics. Maybe your videos got longer. Maybe you had a couple of duds back to back. Maybe you took ten days off and came back.

YouTube builds a picture of who your channel is for. If you post cooking videos for six months and then post one about your truck, the system hands it to cooking people, cooking people swipe, and your retention tanks. Nobody punished you. The system just did what you taught it to do.

The other common one is a run of weak videos. Shorts performance carries a little momentum with it. Two or three duds in a row and your next upload starts from a colder position than it would have a month ago. It recovers. It just takes a few videos.

Am I shadowbanned?

Almost certainly not. I get asked this constantly and in years of doing this I've seen a real restriction maybe a handful of times, and it was always something obvious like copyrighted music or a claim in the video that tripped a policy check.

What people call a shadowban is usually just a Short that failed its test audience. And zero views for the first few minutes after you upload is completely normal, by the way. YouTube has to process the video and run its checks before distribution even starts. Give it an hour before you decide anything is wrong.

If you want to actually rule it out, open YouTube Studio and look for a restriction notice on the video. Check that it's set to public and that you didn't accidentally flag it as made for kids, which kills recommendations. Then search your own title in an incognito window. If none of that turns up anything, you don't have a ban. You have a retention problem.

What actually makes a Short get picked up?

Retention and swipe-through. That's most of it.

The system watches whether people make it to the end, whether they watch it twice, and whether they do anything afterward like liking it or leaving a comment. Loops count for a lot on Shorts because a 20 second video is short enough to watch again without thinking about it.

Which means your first frame does more work than anything else in the video. The first frame, not the first five seconds. Somebody's thumb is already in motion when your video shows up. If the opening image is you standing in front of a beige wall about to say "hey guys, so today I wanted to talk about," they're gone before you finish the sentence.

Start in the middle of something. Show the finished result first and then back into how you got there. Ask the question your video answers as the literal first words out of your mouth. Put text on screen in frame one so the people scrolling with sound off still know what they're looking at.

Length is the other piece nobody wants to hear. A 45 second Short has to hold somebody for 45 seconds to hit full retention. A 22 second Short has to hold them for 22. Shorter videos hit high retention way easier, and high retention is what buys you distribution. If you're stuck, cut everything under 30 seconds for a couple of weeks and watch what it does to your numbers.

Does posting more Shorts fix it?

Sort of, but not the way people mean it.

Posting more helps because you get more shots at the test audience and more real data about what your people respond to. It stops helping the second that extra volume drags your quality down. Ten lazy Shorts a week teaches the system that you run a low retention channel, and that's worse than three good ones.

What matters more is consistency the system can read. Same general subject, same rough rhythm, so YouTube can work out who to hand your stuff to. Three weeks of silence followed by twelve uploads in two days is just noise. It wrecks your own reporting too, because you can't tell what worked.

Three to five a week in the same subject area is a fine target for a local business. If you want a content system you can actually keep up with instead of one that eats your evenings, that's most of what our social media management and content work is built around. And if your TikTok is doing the same thing, I broke that down separately in why your TikTok videos aren't getting views.

What should I do this week?

Open YouTube Studio, pull up your last ten Shorts, and look at the retention graph on each one. Don't look at the view count. Look at where the line drops off.

If the line falls off a cliff in the first three seconds, your openings are the problem. Rewrite them. Delete the intro entirely and start on the payoff.

If the line holds for a while and then sags in the middle, the video is longer than what it's actually delivering. Trim it down.

If the line stays high the whole way through and you're still not getting views, make more of that exact kind of video. You found something that works and the system just hasn't collected enough evidence yet.

Then give it two weeks before you judge any of it. Shorts distribution is slow to update. I've watched client videos sit at 200 views for four days and then pick up 30,000 over a weekend because somebody finally found it and it started moving. That happens way more than people think.

Common questions

How many views is normal for a new YouTube Shorts channel?
A new channel usually sees anywhere from a few dozen to a few hundred views per Short for the first several weeks. That is the test audience doing its job. The number climbs once you have videos that hold attention, not once you cross some subscriber threshold.

Why do my Shorts get zero views right after posting?
That is normal processing. YouTube has to encode the video and run its checks before it shows it to anybody. Wait at least an hour. If you are still at zero after a few hours, make sure the video is set to public and that YouTube Studio is not showing a restriction on it.

Do hashtags help YouTube Shorts get more views?
A little, and less than people think. Hashtags help YouTube figure out what your video is about, so use two or three relevant ones. They will not rescue a video people swipe past. Retention is what decides distribution.

Should I delete my Shorts that got no views?
No. Deleting does not reset anything and you lose the data. A Short that flopped can still get picked up months later, and you need those retention graphs to work out what to change.

Is it too late to start posting YouTube Shorts?
No. Shorts distribution barely cares about channel age or subscriber count, which is why brand new accounts still go viral every day. A channel with zero subscribers and a channel with a hundred thousand get put through the same test.
