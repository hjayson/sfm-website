Why Can't I Boost My Facebook Post?

J
Jayson Hines September 10, 2026 · 7 min read

Short answer: Facebook blocks a boost for one of four reasons. The post type was never eligible, your Page or ad account has a restriction on it, your payment method is broken, or the content tripped an ad policy. If the Boost button is greyed out or says Unavailable, hover over it. Facebook usually tells you which one it is right there in the tooltip, and most people never think to check.

I get this call a couple times a month. Somebody has a post doing well on its own, they go to throw twenty bucks behind it, and nothing happens. Almost every time it's small and fixable. Here's how to figure out which one you're stuck on.

What kinds of posts can Facebook not boost?

Some posts were never eligible in the first place, and this one catches people constantly because the post looks completely normal sitting there on the Page.

You can't boost a post you shared from somebody else. If you hit share on another business's post, even to say something nice about them, that content still belongs to them and Facebook won't let you pay to spread it. You have to make your own post with your own photo or video.

Profile picture changes and new cover photos are out too. Facebook counts those as profile updates rather than real content. Drafts can't be boosted, and neither can a video while you're still live. Wait for the live to end and the replay to save, then you can put money behind the replay. Expired events and old offers get blocked for the obvious reason, which is that Facebook doesn't want you paying to send people to something that already happened.

The sneaky one is tracking junk in your link. If the URL in the post has an fbclid string tacked onto the end, and that happens all the time when you copy a link out of your browser after clicking it from Facebook, the boost gets blocked. Delete everything from the question mark onward, repost the clean link, and it'll go through.

And if you're trying to boost from a personal profile, you can't. Boosting is a Page feature. That one trips up owners who have been running the whole business off their personal account for years.

Is it my Page or my ad account that's restricted?

If the post type is fine and the button still won't work, the block is on you, not the content.

Start with your Page role, because this is the most common one I see with clients who have somebody helping them. Boosting takes an admin or full control role. A moderator or an analyst can respond to comments all day and will never see a working Boost button. Go into Page settings, look at Page access, and check what you actually have.

Next, is the Page even published? An unpublished Page can't advertise at all. Facebook also puts brand new Pages through a short review window where advertising is off, so if you just created the Page this week, that may be all it is.

Then there's the ad account itself. A restricted or disabled ad account kills boosting instantly, and Facebook is not always loud about telling you. Open Ads Manager and look at the Account Quality section. If there's a restriction sitting on the account, no amount of editing the post will help until that gets resolved. I wrote a whole walkthrough on what to do when your Facebook ad account gets disabled if that's where you've landed.

Why does Facebook say my boost was rejected?

Different problem. The boost went through, then came back rejected. That means review looked at it and something in the copy, the image, or the link violated an ad policy.

The rules that catch local businesses most often are the personal attributes ones. Ad copy is not allowed to imply you know something about the person reading it. "Are you struggling with back pain?" gets flagged. "Back pain treatment in Toledo" does not. Same idea, one just doesn't point a finger at the reader.

Health and body claims are the other big bucket. Before and after photos are restricted, weight loss language is restricted, and anything that promises a specific medical result is going to have a bad time. Med spas, chiropractors, and gyms run into this constantly. Housing, employment, and credit ads have their own special category rules that limit targeting, and skipping that step gets the ad pulled.

Meta says most ads finish review within 24 hours. If yours comes back rejected, fix the actual thing that got flagged before you resubmit. Boosting the same post over and over hoping it slips past is how an account picks up a real restriction. There's more detail on reading the rejection notice in my post on why Facebook rejected your ad.

Could it just be a billing problem?

Check this one before you spend an hour rewriting copy. It's boring and it's often the answer.

An expired card, a failed charge from last month, or a bank that flagged Meta as a suspicious merchant will all stop a boost cold. So will hitting your account spending limit, which sits quietly in payment settings and does exactly what it sounds like. Once you hit it, everything stops until you raise it. Go to Billing and Payments in Ads Manager, clear any outstanding balance, update the card, and try again.

If the boost goes live but the money never moves, that's a different animal and I covered it in why your Facebook ads aren't spending.

How do I fix it, step by step?

Work it in this order and you'll find it fast. 1: Hover the Boost button and read the tooltip, since Facebook often names the reason. 2: Confirm the post is yours, isn't a share, isn't a draft, and has no fbclid on the link. 3: Check your Page role is admin or full control. 4: Confirm the Page is published. 5: Open Account Quality in Ads Manager and look for restrictions. 6: Check billing for a failed payment or a spending limit. 7: If it's rejected on policy, rewrite the copy so it isn't talking about the reader personally and isn't promising a result.

If you get through all seven and it's still dead, build the same thing as a real campaign in Ads Manager. Sounds backwards, but Ads Manager gives you actual error messages instead of a greyed out button, and half the time that's how you find out what was wrong.

Should you be boosting at all?

Honest answer, and it's not what you'd expect from somebody who sells this stuff. Boosting is fine for what it is. If a post is already getting real engagement and you want more of your town to see it, twenty or thirty bucks on a boost is a reasonable move.

But boosting is the training wheels version. You get one goal, thin targeting, and reporting that tells you almost nothing about whether anybody called you. Every business I've worked with that was frustrated with Facebook ads was boosting posts and calling it advertising. Once the same budget moves into Ads Manager with a real objective and a real audience, the numbers usually look completely different. Either way, don't let a greyed out button convince you Facebook is broken. It's almost always one of the four things above.

Common questions

Why is the Boost Post button missing on my Facebook page?
Usually it is a permissions or Page status problem. Boosting needs an admin or full-control role on the Page, so a moderator or analyst will not see the button. It also disappears when the Page is unpublished or still in Facebook's new Page review period. Check your role under Page settings first, then check that the Page is actually published.

Can I boost a post I shared from another page?
No. A share belongs to whoever made the original post, so Facebook will not let you put money behind it. Download or recreate the content as your own post with your own image or video and boost that instead, assuming you have the right to use it.

How long does Facebook take to review a boosted post?
Meta says most ads finish review within 24 hours, and plenty clear in under an hour. If yours has been sitting in review longer than a day, do not delete it and start over. Deleting resets the clock. Wait it out, and if it is still stuck after a couple of days, contact Meta support from the Help section in Ads Manager.

Does getting a boost rejected hurt my Facebook page?
One rejection on its own does no damage. Repeated rejections for the same policy are what get an ad account flagged, because Facebook reads that as you ignoring the warning. Fix what got flagged before you resubmit rather than boosting the same post again and hoping it slips through.

Should I be boosting posts at all, or running real ads?
Boost is fine when a post is already getting real engagement and you just want more people to see it. For anything that needs to produce leads or calls, build the campaign in Ads Manager instead. You get proper objectives, audience control, and reporting that tells you what a lead actually cost you.
