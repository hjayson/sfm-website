Am I Shadowbanned on Instagram? How to Check in Two Minutes

J
Jayson Hines August 17, 2026 · 7 min read

Short answer: open Instagram, go to Settings, then Account, then Account Status. If it says your account isn't eligible to be recommended, you've got the real thing. If it says everything's fine, your reach problem is something else, and that's the far more common answer.

I get asked this constantly, usually by somebody whose views fell off a cliff two weeks ago and who's now read four different blog posts telling them to delete the app for 48 hours. Account Status takes about two minutes to check and it settles the question. Do that before you do anything drastic.

Is the Instagram shadowban even real?

Instagram spent years saying no. Then they built a screen that tells you whether your content can be recommended, which is the same thing wearing a different name.

The company's word for it is "non-recommendable." What that means in practice is your posts still exist, your followers can still see them in their feed, and nothing looks broken from where you're sitting. What's gone is distribution to people who don't follow you. No Explore page, no Reels tab for strangers, no hashtag surfacing. For most business accounts that's where the majority of new eyeballs came from, so it feels like the account died even though nothing was deleted.

So it's real. It's just narrower than the internet makes it sound, and it's not the reason most accounts lose reach.

How do I check if I'm shadowbanned?

Account Status first. Settings, then Account, then Account Status. Instagram will tell you in plain words whether your content is eligible to be recommended and whether anything you've posted has been flagged. That's the closest thing to an official answer you're ever going to get, and it beats every third-party "shadowban checker" site by a mile.

Those checker tools, by the way, are mostly guessing. Some want your login, which you should never hand over.

If you want a second read, there's a manual test that actually works. Post something using a specific hashtag that has under 50,000 posts, so a small niche one rather than a giant one. Wait half an hour. Then log out, or grab a phone that's never followed you, and search that hashtag. If your post shows up in neither Recent nor Top, your hashtag visibility is restricted. If it shows up, it isn't.

The other thing worth doing is opening your own Insights and looking at the split between followers and non-followers on recent posts. If reach from non-followers went to nearly nothing while your follower reach held steady, that pattern lines up with being non-recommendable. If both dropped together, you're looking at a content problem, not a penalty.

What actually causes it?

The biggest one right now is engagement pods and coordinated commenting. If you're in a group chat where everybody likes and comments on each other's posts on a schedule, Instagram's systems read that tight repetitive loop as fake amplification. It's become the leading trigger, and a lot of people in these groups think they're doing legitimate networking.

Behaving like a bot does it too. Following and unfollowing forty accounts an hour, pasting the same comment under dozens of posts, mass-DMing. Automation tools that promise growth are usually doing exactly this on your behalf, which is why buying followers or engagement so often ends with reach collapsing a month later.

Then there's content that sits right on the line without crossing it. Not rule-breaking, just close enough that the automated systems get cautious. Certain health and weight loss claims, anything that reads as adult, some firearms and alcohol content, and a lot of "make money fast" language. Plenty of legitimate businesses get caught here. A med spa posting before-and-after body photos runs into this all the time and hasn't done anything wrong.

Broken or banned hashtags round it out. Tags get flagged when spam floods them, including tags that sound completely harmless, and using one can drag the whole post down with it.

My reach dropped but Account Status says I'm fine. Now what?

Then you're not shadowbanned, and honestly this describes most people who ask me. That's good news, because a content problem is something you control.

Usually it's watch time. Reels live and die on whether people stay past the first couple seconds, and if your opening is slow, the test audience bails and Instagram stops showing it around. It can also be that you changed what you post about, so the audience Instagram had built for you no longer matches the work. Or you went quiet for three weeks and the momentum's just gone.

Seasonality is real too. Mid-summer and the week around a holiday are quiet for almost everybody, and people panic about a dip that had nothing to do with them.

I went through the whole diagnostic in why your Instagram reach is dropping, which is the better read if Account Status came back clean.

How do I fix a shadowban?

Start by stopping whatever caused it, because nothing else matters until that's done. Leave the pods. Turn off any automation or growth tool you're running. If Account Status flagged a specific post, delete it, and if you think the flag was wrong, appeal it right there in that screen. Instagram does overturn these.

Then go through your last month or so of posts and pull the hashtags. Search each one inside the app. If a tag brings back a notice or shows almost nothing, it's compromised, so drop it from your rotation and don't use it again.

Then ease off for a bit. Post less for a week and keep your liking and following at a normal human pace. You're waiting out a cooldown, and you can't rush it.

Then get back to posting well. Original video, made for the app rather than a TikTok with the watermark still on it, with a first two seconds that earn the next ten. Reply to comments in the app like a person. Restrictions usually lift on their own inside a couple weeks once the behavior stops.

What doesn't work, despite what you'll read: deleting the app, switching to a personal account and back, or posting ten times a day to "reset the algorithm." None of that does anything, and the last one can make it worse.

How long does it last?

Usually a couple weeks, assuming you actually stopped doing the thing. Some accounts see it clear in a few days. If you're a month out and Account Status still says you're not recommendable, something's still active, so go back through your recent posts and your tools rather than waiting it out longer.

Recovery is a ramp, not a switch. Reach comes back gradually as the system re-learns who to show your work to, so don't panic on day three when it's still slow.

And if the whole thing has you rethinking how much of your business sits on rented land, that's a reasonable takeaway. One policy change and your entire lead flow can go quiet through no fault of your own. Common questions

How do I check if I am shadowbanned on Instagram?
Open Instagram and go to Settings, then Account, then Account Status. Instagram will tell you plainly whether your content is eligible to be recommended and whether any post has been flagged. That is the closest thing to an official answer, and it is far more reliable than any third party shadowban checker.

Does Instagram admit shadowbans exist?
Not in those words. Instagram calls it content being non-recommendable, which means your posts stay visible to your followers but stop being distributed to people who do not follow you. Different name, same effect on your reach.

How long does an Instagram shadowban last?
Most restrictions clear within about two weeks once you stop whatever triggered them. Some lift in a few days. If you are a month out and Account Status still says you are not recommendable, something is probably still running, so audit your recent posts and any automation tools.

Can banned hashtags get my account restricted?
Yes. Hashtags get flagged when spammers flood them, including tags that sound completely harmless, and using one can suppress the whole post. Search each hashtag inside the app before you use it. If it returns a warning or almost no recent posts, leave it alone.

Will deleting the Instagram app fix a shadowban?
No. Deleting the app, switching between personal and business accounts, or posting far more often are all popular advice that does nothing. Restrictions lift when the behavior that caused them stops, not when you reinstall.
