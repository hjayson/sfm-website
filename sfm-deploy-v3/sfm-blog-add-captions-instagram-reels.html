How Do I Add Captions to Instagram Reels?

J
Jayson Hines October 19, 2026 · 5 min read

To add captions to Instagram Reels, open your video in the Reel editor, tap the sticker button, choose Captions, then review the words before sharing. If that sticker isn't there, you can add subtitles in a video editor before you upload. Instagram moves things around, so the labels on your version may look a little different.

Captions help people follow a Reel when they can't turn the sound on. They also make spoken details easier to catch. A quick review matters, though. Auto captions can miss names and local place names.

How do you add captions to an Instagram Reel?

Start a Reel with a video you recorded or choose a clip from your camera roll. In the editing screen, open the sticker tray and tap Captions. Instagram will process the speech and place the words on the video. Give it a moment if the text doesn't appear right away.

Read the captions from beginning to end. Fix names or business terms, and correct any line that changes the meaning. Then choose a style and move the text where people can read it without covering a face or an important part of the clip.

Before you publish, play the Reel once with the sound off. If the main point still makes sense, the captions are doing their job.

How do you fix words Instagram transcribes wrong?

Auto captions are a first pass. They may mishear a name, a product, or a word said quickly. Tap into the caption text and correct it before sharing. This small check is easy to skip when you are posting in a hurry. It is also the part viewers notice when it goes wrong.

If your video uses music under speech, make sure the voice is clear enough to hear. Lower background music while you edit, or record the voiceover again in a quiet room. Captions can't rescue a line that's hard to make out.

What can you do if the Captions sticker is missing?

First, update Instagram and reopen the Reel editor. Look through the full sticker tray. The app can change where features appear, and some tools may not be available for every account or language.

If it still isn't there, add subtitles with the video editor you already use, then upload the finished video. Watch the export before posting to catch timing or spelling problems. Keep a clean copy of the original clip too, in case you want to make another version later.

Should you use Instagram captions or subtitles added before upload?

Use Instagram's Captions sticker when it's available and the automatic transcript is close. It's quick, and you can fix the text in the app. Add subtitles before upload when you need more control over the wording, font, or timing.

Either way, choose one caption track and check the final video. Seeing the same words twice can make a Reel harder to follow. For a local business, it is usually better to keep the style simple and let the person, product, or work stay visible.

Where should captions sit on a Reel?

Keep the words away from the bottom edge and the right side of the frame. Instagram's buttons, account name, and description can sit there. A viewer should not have to guess at the last word because a control covered it.

Use readable type and enough contrast with the picture behind it. Break a long sentence into shorter lines, but do not flash them so quickly that people cannot keep up. If the video has a lot happening, move the captions to a quieter part of the frame.

Common questions

Where do I find captions when making a Reel?
Open the Reel editor and look in the sticker tray for Captions. Review the generated words before sharing.

Why is the Captions sticker missing?
Update Instagram and check again. If it still is not available on your account, add subtitles in a video editor before uploading.

Can I edit Instagram auto captions?
Yes. Read the text and correct any words Instagram heard incorrectly, especially names and places.

Should I use captions or text overlays?
Captions show the spoken words. A short overlay can add context, but keep it separate and easy to read.

Where should captions sit on a Reel?
Keep them away from the bottom and right edge so Instagram's controls do not cover the text.
