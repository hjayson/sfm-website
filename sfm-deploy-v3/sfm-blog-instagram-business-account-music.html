Why Can't I Use Music on My Instagram Business Account?

J
Jayson Hines September 11, 2026 · 6 min read

Short answer: nothing is broken. Instagram pulled the full song catalog away from you the moment your account was set to Business. Business accounts get the Meta Sound Collection instead, which is around 14,000 tracks that are actually cleared for commercial use. Personal accounts get everything, you get the cleared stuff.

I get this question from local business owners constantly. They watch a competitor post a Reel with whatever song is everywhere that week, go to make their own, and the search bar comes up almost empty. Then they spend an hour looking for the setting that turned it off. There isn't one.

So why does Instagram do this?

It comes down to who Meta paid, and for what.

Meta has licensing deals with the major record labels. Those deals cover personal and creator use, someone posting a video of their kid with a song over it. They do not cover a business using that same song to sell roofing, or med spa packages, or lunch specials. That is commercial use, and commercial use is a separate and much more expensive kind of license that Meta did not buy on your behalf.

So when Instagram sees a Business account, it shows you the pile it can legally show you. That is the whole mechanic. It has nothing to do with your follower count, your location, or how long you've had the account.

Worth saying plainly: a song showing up inside Instagram's library does not mean you have the right to use it commercially. The library is a convenience, not a license.

Should I just switch to a Creator account?

This is the workaround everybody finds within about ten minutes of googling, and yes, it usually works. Flip to Creator and most of the catalog comes back.

But think about what you're actually doing. Instagram is treating you as a person instead of a company, and the copyright question doesn't change just because the app stopped blocking you. If you're running a business and using a Taylor Swift track to advertise your service, the label can still come after the business. Warner Music sued a makeup brand called Iconic London in 2022 over more than 165 songs they'd used across Instagram and TikTok. That's not a hypothetical.

You also give things up. Creator accounts lose some of the business features, and depending on what you use, scheduling tools and certain shopping options get weird. For a local plumber or a dentist it's usually not worth the trade.

Now, I'm not going to pretend nobody does it. Small local accounts using a popular song on an occasional Reel almost never hear anything beyond the audio getting muted. Just go in knowing that's the deal you're taking, instead of thinking the Creator switch made it legal.

What actually happens if I use a trending song anyway?

Usually one of these. The audio gets muted and your Reel plays silent, which looks worse than posting nothing. Or the post gets removed. Or it stays up but gets blocked in certain countries, which quietly kills your reach.

Detection also got a lot faster. Stuff that slid through two years ago now gets flagged within seconds of posting. And repeat copyright hits on an account can cost you Reels access or music access entirely, which is a real problem if video is how you get found. If your Reels already aren't getting seen, I wrote about why Instagram Reels get no views separately, and muted audio is one of the quieter causes.

What should a local business use instead?

The Meta Sound Collection is better than its reputation. The trick is that most people open it, see the front page, decide it's all elevator music, and close it. Search by mood or by tempo instead. Save anything decent to your favorites the first time you hear it. After a couple of weeks you've got your own short list and picking audio takes ten seconds.

Original audio is the other answer, and honestly it's the better one. You talking over your own footage outperforms a trending song for most local businesses anyway, because the algorithm is looking for watch time and a person explaining something holds attention longer than a clip with a pop song stapled to it. Nobody stopped scrolling for your roofing video because of the beat.

If you want current-sounding music with real commercial rights, subscription libraries handle it. Epidemic Sound and Artlist both license for commercial social use and run in the range of a few hundred dollars a year. You upload the track with your video rather than grabbing it inside the app. For a business posting several videos a week, that pays for itself the first time a Reel doesn't get muted.

One more thing that gets missed: trending audio and trending music aren't the same. A lot of what goes around on Reels is a voice clip, a sound effect, or somebody's original audio that other people started using. Those are often fine and nobody in your market is using them. That's where I'd spend the hunting time.

The part that matters more than the song

I've sat with owners who were genuinely stuck on this for weeks, convinced the music was the reason their videos flopped. It almost never is. The first second of the video is the reason. What you say is the reason. Whether you posted twice or eleven times last month is the reason.

Music is a garnish. If you want an outside read on what your account is actually doing wrong, that's part of what we do with social media management for Northwest Ohio businesses, and it usually takes about fifteen minutes to spot.

Common questions

Why did the music disappear from my Instagram business account?
Nothing broke. When your account is set to Business, Instagram swaps the full music catalog for the Meta Sound Collection, which is the set of tracks cleared for commercial use. Meta's licensing deals with the record labels cover personal and creator use, not businesses promoting themselves.

Will switching to a Creator account give me the music back?
Usually yes, and plenty of local businesses do it. Just know that Instagram treats a Creator account as a person, not a company, so if you are using a song to sell your service you are still the one on the hook if a label complains. You also lose some of the business tools and scheduling options.

What happens if I use a trending song anyway?
Most of the time the audio gets muted or the post gets pulled, sometimes minutes after you post it. Detection got a lot faster in 2026. Rights holders can also come after the business directly. Warner Music sued the makeup brand Iconic London in 2022 over more than 165 songs used across Instagram and TikTok.

Is the Meta Sound Collection actually usable?
It is better than people expect. There are roughly 14,000 tracks and the newer additions sound current. Search by mood instead of scrolling the front page, save the ones you like, and you will build a small set you can reuse.

Can I just use music I bought or a song from a licensing site?
Yes, if the license covers commercial social media use. Buying a song on iTunes does not give you that right. Subscription libraries like Epidemic Sound and Artlist do, and you upload the audio with your video instead of picking it from the Instagram library.
