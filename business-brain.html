Block 1 · The Spine

Teach it who you are

Stop talking to a stranger. Give your brain a foundation.
Prompt 1 · The Stranger
Type this into a blank chat first. This is the "before." Don't add anything to it.
Write a Facebook post for my business about [what you're promoting].
Prompt 2 · The Interview
This is the one. Paste it and answer the questions. Six is the floor, keep going if you want.
You're about to become the brain of my business. Not a chatbot I bark orders at, a second brain that knows my business almost as well as I do. Right now you know nothing about me. So before you write a single word for me, you're going to learn my business the way a sharp consultant would on day one. Interview me. One question at a time, and wait for my answer before the next one. Start with the 6 questions that teach you the most about: - who I am and why I started this - who I serve and what they're really struggling with - what I sell and what makes me different - what I stand for, the values I won't compromise on - how I talk If an answer is thin, dig once, then move on. If I get stuck, hand me a few options to react to so I'm never staring at a blank screen. Make one of those questions count: ask me to paste something I've actually written, an email, a post, a reply to a customer. Study it. Learn my voice, my rhythm, the words I use and the ones I'd never be caught saying. When you write as me later, I shouldn't be able to tell it wasn't me. Six questions is just the floor. When you've covered them, ask me if I want to keep going deeper or wrap up. If I want to keep talking, follow my lead. The more I give you, the sharper you get. When I'm ready, stop asking and write me a "Business Brain Profile" with these sections: Who I Am, Who I Serve, What I Sell, What Makes Me Different, What I Stand For, My Voice, and Never Do This. Tight enough that I can paste it into any AI and it instantly gets me. Start with question one. Just one.
Prompt 3 · The Wrap-Up
Running long? Paste this to make it stop and build your profile.
We've covered enough. Stop interviewing me and write my Business Brain Profile now, based on everything I've told you so far, using those same sections.
Then paste Prompt 1 again into the same chat. Watch the difference. Save your profile somewhere safe so it survives the drive home.
Block 2 · The Hands

Put it to work

Give your brain a home, then make it do the task you hate.
Put it to work
Run this inside your new Project, where your profile lives.
Using everything you know about my business, do this for me: [describe the task]. Sound like me, not like a robot. Give it your best shot. This is a first draft we'll sharpen together.
The Coaching Palette
The first answer is never the final answer. Direct it like an employee. Tap any line to copy.
  • Make it shorter. Cut it in half.
  • More casual, like I'm talking to a friend.
  • More professional, but still human.
  • Punchier. Stronger hook in the first line.
  • Give me 3 different versions.
  • Rewrite it the way I'd actually say it.
  • Make it sound less like AI.
  • Add a clear call to action at the end.
Build your brain a home
Stop living in one endless chat. Make a container so it remembers you. Find yours below (menus shift, so look for the closest match).

ChatGPT

Projects (or Custom GPTs). Make one, paste your profile into its instructions, and work inside it.

Claude

Projects. Create a Project, drop your profile into the project knowledge, and chat inside it.

Gemini

Gems. Open the Gem manager, create a Gem, paste your profile into the instructions.

Copilot

Notebooks. Create a Notebook with your profile and ground Copilot on it.

Block 3 · The Nervous System

Wire it into your whole business

A brain for the three things that eat your week: follow-up, email, and your calendar.
The Follow-Up Brain
Where most owners lose money. Fix it first.
You're my follow-up assistant. I'll tell you about a client or a conversation, and you'll write the follow-up for me, in my voice, that moves things forward without being pushy. Here's the situation: [who they are, what we talked about, what I want to happen next]. Write the follow-up message. Then give me a shorter version I could send as a text.
The Email Brain
Triage the pile, then draft the replies that matter.
You're my email assistant. Here's an email I got: [paste it]. Draft my reply in my voice. I want to say: [your gist, or "help me figure out what to say"]. Keep it [warm / brief / firm]. Then suggest a subject line.
The Calendar & Planning Brain
Hand it the chaos. Get back a real week.
You're my planning assistant. Here's everything on my plate this week: [dump your to-dos, meetings, deadlines]. Build me a realistic week, day by day. Protect time for [the thing that actually grows my business]. Tell me what to drop or move if it's too much.

Take It Home · The Heartbeat

Feed your brain

A brain is only as good as what you feed it. Keep it alive past today.

Five ways to feed it

  1. Every time it nails something, save the prompt. That's your library.
  2. Every time it misses, tell it why. Inside a Project, it remembers.
  3. Paste in your wins: a great email, a post that worked, a happy review. That's voice training.
  4. Run deep research on your own business. See yourself from the outside. Fix the gap.
  5. Build one new brain a week. Follow-up, then email, then calendar, then the next.
You are the heartbeat. The brain handles the busywork so you can do the work only you can do.
